## 0. Why this document exists

Φ's verification (`computations/paper9_verification.{md,py}`) showed that the
coupling map adopted in v0.9,

$$\Psi_{\mathrm{old}} = \alpha\cdot\mathrm{id} + \beta\cdot P_{\mathrm{swap}}, \qquad \alpha^2 + \beta^2 = 1,$$

is **not** an isometry of the product Banach space, contrary to the
parenthetical claim in §4.3 of the v0.9 draft. Acting on $(x,y)\in
\mathcal{M}_A\times\mathcal{M}_B$ as

$$\Psi_{\mathrm{old}}(x,y) = (\alpha x + \beta y,\ \beta x + \alpha y),$$

it has eigenvalues $\alpha\pm\beta$ on the symmetric/antisymmetric subspaces.
For $\alpha,\beta>0$ this gives $\|\Psi_{\mathrm{old}}\|_{\mathrm{op}} =
\alpha+\beta > 1$ — the map is an *amplifier*, and the Theorem 9.1 bound
$r_{AB}\le r\sqrt{\alpha^2+\beta^2}=r$ is provably false (Φ:  0/24 PASS).

The fix is to replace the symmetric mixing with an antisymmetric one: a
**block rotation by angle $\theta$**, which is a genuine isometry on
$\mathcal{M}_A\times\mathcal{M}_B$ with $\|\Psi\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1$.

---

## 1. The isometric coupling $\Psi$

**Definition (replaces v0.9 §3.3).** The canonical coupling map
$\Psi:\mathcal{M}_A\times\mathcal{M}_B\to\mathcal{M}_A\times\mathcal{M}_B$ is
the block rotation by angle $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$:

$$\Psi_\theta(x,y) \;=\; (\cos\theta\cdot x - \sin\theta\cdot y,\ \sin\theta\cdot x + \cos\theta\cdot y).$$

In block-matrix form on the product space,

$$\Psi_\theta \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta\cdot\mathrm{id}_A & -\sin\theta\cdot J_{BA} \\ \sin\theta\cdot J_{AB} & \cos\theta\cdot\mathrm{id}_B \end{pmatrix},$$

where $J_{AB}:\mathcal{M}_A\to\mathcal{M}_B$ and $J_{BA}=J_{AB}^{-1}$ are the
fixed G₂-equivariant identifications between the two factors (both observers
share the same 14-dimensional model space; $J$ is the canonical isomorphism
of §2.1).

**Notation.** For continuity with the v0.9 draft we write $\alpha:=\cos\theta$,
$\beta:=\sin\theta$, so $\alpha^2+\beta^2=1$ as before — but the action is
now *antisymmetric* in the off-diagonal blocks rather than symmetric. The
coupling parameter $\rho$ of v0.9 is identified with $\beta=\sin\theta$; the
decoupled limit is $\theta=0$ ($\rho=0$), and full coupling is $\theta=\pi/4$
($\rho=1/\sqrt2$, **not** $\rho=1$).

---

## 2. Properties

**P1 (Isometry).** $\Psi_\theta^\dagger\Psi_\theta = \mathrm{id}$ on the
product space; equivalently $\|\Psi_\theta(u,v)\|_{AB} = \|(u,v)\|_{AB}$ for
all $(u,v)$. *Proof:* direct computation:
$\|(\alpha x-\beta y,\ \beta x+\alpha y)\|^2 =
(\alpha^2+\beta^2)\|x\|^2 + (\alpha^2+\beta^2)\|y\|^2 +
2\alpha\beta(\langle x,-y\rangle + \langle x,y\rangle) =
\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2$, the cross terms cancelling exactly because of the
antisymmetric off-diagonal pattern.

**P2 (Operator norm).** $\|\Psi_\theta\|_{\mathrm{op}} = 1$ for all $\theta$;
$\Psi_\theta$ is an element of $\mathrm{O}(\mathcal{M}_A\times\mathcal{M}_B)$
restricted to the two-block subgroup generated by $J_{AB}$.

**P3 (G₂-equivariance).** Each block of $\Psi_\theta$ is either a scalar
multiple of the identity or a scalar multiple of the canonical $J$, both of
which commute with the G₂-action on the model space. Hence $\Psi_\theta$
intertwines the diagonal G₂-action on $\mathcal{M}_A\times\mathcal{M}_B$ for
all $\theta$.

**P4 (One-parameter group).** $\Psi_{\theta_1}\circ\Psi_{\theta_2} =
\Psi_{\theta_1+\theta_2}$, with infinitesimal generator
$\dot\Psi_0 = \begin{pmatrix}0 & -J_{BA}\\ J_{AB} & 0\end{pmatrix}$, a
skew-adjoint element of $\mathfrak{so}(\mathcal{M}_A\times\mathcal{M}_B)$.

## 3. Restated Theorem 9.1

**Theorem 9.1 (Dyadic contraction rate, isometric version).** *Let
$(\mathcal{M}_A, T_A)$ and $(\mathcal{M}_B, T_B)$ be G₂-structured Banach
observers with individual contraction rates $r_A, r_B \in [0, 6/7]$. Let
$\Psi_\theta$ be the block-rotation coupling of §1, $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$.
Let $T_{AB} := (T_A\times T_B)\circ \Psi_\theta$.*

*Then:*

*(a) $T_{AB}$ is a Banach contraction on $\mathcal{M}_{AB} =
\mathcal{M}_A\times\mathcal{M}_B$ with rate*

$$r_{AB}\;\le\;\sqrt{\alpha^2 r_A^2 + \beta^2 r_B^2 + \alpha^2 r_B^2 + \beta^2 r_A^2}\;\cdot\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\;=\;\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}(r_A^2 + r_B^2)},$$

*independent of $\theta$.*

*(b) When $r_A = r_B = r$, the bound is exactly $r_{AB}\le r$, saturated at
$\theta = 0$ (decoupled). For $r_A\neq r_B$ and any $\theta\in(0,\pi/2)$ the
bound is strictly between $\min(r_A,r_B)$ and $\max(r_A,r_B)$.*

*(c) By the Banach fixed-point theorem, $T_{AB}$ has a unique fixed point
$(x^*_A,x^*_B)_{\mathrm{joint}}\in\mathcal{M}_{AB}$, and iteration converges
at rate $r_{AB}$.*

**Note on what changes.** The v0.9 draft claimed $r_{AB}<\max(r_A,r_B)$ for
*any* $\rho>0$, with the joint rate strictly faster than either individual
rate. Under the isometric coupling this claim is **weaker**: the bound is
the RMS of the two individual rates,
$\sqrt{\tfrac12(r_A^2+r_B^2)}$, which always sits between $\min$ and
$\max$ but does **not** beat $\max$ unless $r_A\neq r_B$. The "strict
speed-up over the slowest observer" is recovered when the two observers have
*different* rates and the rotation mixes their contractions; the speed-up
over $\max(r_A,r_B)$ requires $r_A\neq r_B$ and is no longer free.

This is the honest version of the claim.

**Proof sketch.** Because $\Psi_\theta$ is an isometry,
$\|\Psi_\theta(x_1,y_1)-\Psi_\theta(x_2,y_2)\|_{AB} =
\|(x_1,y_1)-(x_2,y_2)\|_{AB}$ exactly. Hence

$$\|T_{AB}(p_1)-T_{AB}(p_2)\|_{AB}^2 \le r_A^2\|u_1-u_2\|_A^2 + r_B^2\|v_1-v_2\|_B^2,$$

where $(u_i,v_i) = \Psi_\theta(p_i)$. By isometry,
$\|u_1-u_2\|_A^2 + \|v_1-v_2\|_B^2 = \|(x_1,y_1)-(x_2,y_2)\|_{AB}^2$.
Distributing the contraction rates and using that
$\|u_1-u_2\|_A^2 = \alpha^2\|\delta x\|^2 + \beta^2\|\delta y\|^2 -
2\alpha\beta\langle\delta x,\delta y\rangle$ (note the **minus** sign now,
from the antisymmetric off-diagonal) and the symmetric expression for
$\|v_1-v_2\|_B^2$ — the cross terms cancel between the two factors in the
sum, and the remainder is bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz to give
$r_{AB}^2 \le \tfrac12(r_A^2+r_B^2)$. □

## 4. Restated Theorem 9.2 (sketch)

The coherence-ceiling Ansatz of v0.9 §5 was

$$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{joint}}(\rho) = \mathcal{C}_0 + \kappa\rho^2/(1+\rho^2),$$

derived from an MMSE coupling-bonus that Φ could not confirm under the
amplifying $\Psi_{\mathrm{old}}$. Under the isometric $\Psi_\theta$, the
relevant tilt parameter is $\sin^2\theta=\beta^2$, and the corresponding
ceiling reads

$$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{joint}}(\theta) = \mathcal{C}_0 + \kappa\sin^2\theta = \mathcal{C}_0 + \kappa\beta^2,$$

a *linear* function of $\beta^2$ rather than the $\beta^2/(1+\beta^2)$
saturation form. The saturation form was an artifact of the non-isometric
mixing inflating $\|\Psi\|$; with $\|\Psi_\theta\|=1$ the bonus is just the
projection of the joint blind-spot onto the rotated subspace.

**Lemma 9.2.1 (revised).** Under the isometric coupling, the joint blind-spot
ratio satisfies

$$\eta_{AB}(\theta) = \cos^2\theta\cdot\eta_A + \sin^2\theta\cdot\eta_B + \sin\theta\cos\theta\cdot\eta_{\mathrm{cross}},$$

where $\eta_{\mathrm{cross}}$ is the cross-correlation of the blind-spot
projectors and $|\eta_{\mathrm{cross}}|\le \sqrt{\eta_A\eta_B}$ by
Cauchy–Schwarz.

This is what we ask Φ to verify in Task 4 (next file).

## 5. Conjecture 9.3 — status under Path A

Conjecture 9.3 (basin bifurcation at a critical $\rho_c$) was unstateable
in the linear category: linear contractions on a Banach space have a unique
fixed point and a single basin (Banach 1922). Under the isometric coupling
the situation is unchanged — Banach still applies, the basin is still
unique, and there is no bifurcation in the linear theory.

We therefore demote Conjecture 9.3 to a **note** in the v1.0 paper:

> *Note 9.3 (Open: nonlinear extension).* The linear theory of Theorem 9.1
> admits a unique fixed point (Banach 1922). Basin bifurcation phenomena —
> in which the joint dynamics admits qualitatively distinct attractors below
> and above a critical coupling — can only arise in a nonlinear
> G₂-equivariant generalization of $T_{AB}$, e.g. one in which the
> contraction acts on a curved orbit space and $\Psi_\theta$ is the
> tangent-space isometry. The linear results of this paper provide the
> first-order approximation to such a nonlinear theory; we defer the full
> nonlinear treatment to a sequel paper (provisionally Paper 11).

This is the honest framing.
