# Paper 9 v1.2 — Math Correctness Review (GPT-5.5)

## Summary verdict
Reject in current form — the rate-channel negative result is largely salvageable, but the affine-channel section contains wrong algebra, a sign error in the perturbation expansion, and a Proposition 9.5 that is not proved and is false under allowed hypotheses.

## Issue inventory

### Issue 1: Theorem 9.1 needs positivity and Hilbert-space hypotheses
**Location:** §4.1, Theorem 9.1; §3.3.2 remark
**Severity:** Minor
**Description:** The spectral calculation for \(\Psi_{\mathrm{sym}}=\alpha I+\beta P_{\mathrm{swap}}\) is correct only after adding the missing assumptions that the product space is being treated as a Hilbert/Euclidean direct sum with the canonical swap isometry and that \(\alpha,\beta\ge 0\). As written, \(\alpha^2+\beta^2=1\) and \(\beta>0\) do not imply \(\alpha+\beta>1\); for negative \(\alpha\), the norm is \(\max(|\alpha+eta|,|\alpha-eta|)\), not necessarily \(\alpha+eta\).
**Suggested fix:** State \(\alpha=\cos\rho\), \(\beta=\sin\rho\), \(0<\rho<\pi/2\), or explicitly assume \(\alpha,\beta>0\). Also state that the norm is the \(G_2\)-invariant Euclidean norm on \(V^{14}\oplus V^{14}\), not an arbitrary Banach norm.

### Issue 2: Theorem 9.1's lower-bound proof is only sketched
**Location:** §4.1, proof of Theorem 9.1, lines 638–645
**Severity:** Minor
**Description:** The conclusion \(r_{AB}^{\mathrm{sym}}\ge r_-(\alpha+eta)>r_-\) is true under the added positivity assumptions, but the proof does not actually show it. It jumps from the eigendirections of \(\Psi_{\mathrm{sym}}\) to the equal-rate case. For unequal rates the symmetric eigendirection is not generally an eigenvector of \((T_A\oplus T_B)\Psi_{\mathrm{sym}}\).
**Suggested fix:** Insert the short argument: for any unit symmetric vector \(u=(x,x)/\sqrt2\), \(\|\Psi_{\mathrm{sym}}u\|=(\alpha+eta)\), and since \(D=\operatorname{diag}(r_A I,r_B I)\) satisfies \(\|Dz\|\ge r_-\|z\|\), \(\|D\Psi_{\mathrm{sym}}u\|\ge r_-(\alpha+eta)\). Then separately note equality when \(r_A=r_B=r\).

### Issue 3: Lemma 3.3.1 is not valid in the stated Banach generality
**Location:** §3.3 and §3.3.1, especially lines 525–538
**Severity:** Major
**Description:** The isometry proof expands squares and uses \(\langle x,y\rangle\). That is Hilbert-space reasoning, not Banach-space reasoning. It also implicitly identifies \(x\in\mathcal M_A\) and \(y\in\mathcal M_B\) in a common inner-product space. Without an explicitly fixed isometric identification \(J_{AB}:\mathcal M_A\to\mathcal M_B\) and an inner product, the cross term \(\langle x,y\rangle\) is not defined and the cancellation proof is meaningless.
**Suggested fix:** Replace “Banach product norm” here by “the Euclidean norm induced by a fixed \(G_2\)-invariant inner product on the adjoint representation, transported by a fixed \(G_2\)-equivariant isometry \(J_{AB}\).” If the paper wants genuine Banach spaces, prove isometry by a norm-preserving 2D rotation structure, not by inner products.

### Issue 4: The claimed uniqueness of the block-rotation family is false or unproved
**Location:** §3.6 lines 486–492; §3.3.2 lines 585–588
**Severity:** Minor
**Description:** The paper claims the block rotation is the unique isometric \(G_2\)-equivariant family reducing to the identity. The commutant of the diagonal action on \(V\oplus V\) is \(M_2(\mathbb R)\otimes I_V\), so the isometric \(G_2\)-equivariant maps form \(O(2)\) acting on the two observer labels. There are many paths in \(O(2)\), and even one-parameter subgroups can be reparameterized.
**Suggested fix:** Downgrade to: “a canonical one-parameter rotation family in the \(SO(2)\) part of the \(G_2\)-equivariant isometry group.” If uniqueness is needed, specify the normalization and classify the allowed \(O(2)\) paths.

### Issue 5: Theorem 9.2's upper bound is correct, but the tightness proof is wrong
**Location:** §4.2, proof of Theorem 9.2, lines 679–692
**Severity:** Major
**Description:** The submultiplicative upper bound \(\|T_{AB}\|\le\max(r_A,r_B)\) is correct. The exhibited vector \((x_0,0)\) only attains \(r_A\) at \(\theta=0\); for \(\theta>0\) it gives \((r_A^2\cos^2\theta+r_B^2\sin^2\theta)^{1/2}\), which is generally strictly below \(\max(r_A,r_B)\) when \(r_A\ne r_B\). The sentence about a “weighted combination” is not a proof.
**Suggested fix:** Use unitary/orthogonal invariance of singular values. Write \(D=\operatorname{diag}(r_A I,r_B I)\). Since \(\Psi_\theta\) is orthogonal, \(\|D\Psi_\theta\|=\|D\|=\max(r_A,r_B)\). A maximizing unit vector is \(\Psi_\theta^{-1}(v,0)\) if \(r_A\ge r_B\), or \(\Psi_\theta^{-1}(0,v)\) if \(r_B\ge r_A\).

### Issue 6: Theorem 9.2 is applied later outside its stated hypotheses
**Location:** §5.2 lines 906–909; Theorem 9.4(a,d), lines 920–943 and 965
**Severity:** Major
**Description:** Theorem 9.2 is stated only for scalar maps \(T_A=r_A I\), \(T_B=r_B I\). The affine section uses general orthogonal \(R_A,R_B\) and repeatedly invokes Theorem 9.2 to conclude \(\|L_\theta\|=\max(r_A,r_B)\). This conclusion is true for orthogonal \(R_A,R_B\), but it is not a consequence of Theorem 9.2 as stated and it is not a Schur consequence.
**Suggested fix:** Add a lemma before Theorem 9.4: for \(L_\theta=\operatorname{diag}(r_A R_A,r_B R_B)\Psi_\theta\) with \(R_A,R_B\) orthogonal, \(\|L_\theta\|=\max(r_A,r_B)\). Proof: left multiplication by \(\operatorname{diag}(R_A,R_B)\) and right multiplication by \(\Psi_\theta\) are orthogonal, leaving the singular values of \(\operatorname{diag}(r_A I,r_B I)\).

### Issue 7: Corollary 9.3 overstates real Schur's lemma
**Location:** §4.3, Corollary 9.3, lines 715–726
**Severity:** Minor
**Description:** For the 14-dimensional adjoint representation of compact \(G_2\), the claim \(\operatorname{End}_{G_2}(V^{14})=\mathbb R\) is correct. The corollary as written, however, says every nontrivial irreducible real representation has scalar commutant. Real Schur's lemma only gives a real division algebra \(\mathbb R\), \(\mathbb C\), or \(\mathbb H\); the scalar conclusion requires proving the representation is of real type. The proof only justifies the 7- and 14-dimensional cases.
**Suggested fix:** State the corollary only for the adjoint \(V^{14}\), or add a representation-theoretic argument that the specific class of \(G_2\)-modules under discussion is of real type.

### Issue 8: The affine equivariance discussion is conceptually right but internally inconsistent
**Location:** §2.1 lines 237–247; §5.1 lines 857–886; §4.4 lines 752–778
**Severity:** Major
**Description:** The statement “\(b\ne0\) breaks full \(G_2\)-equivariance because the only fixed vector is 0” is correct. But full equivariance of \(T(x)=rRx+b\) also requires \(R\) to commute with \(G_2\) when \(r\ne0\). The paper sometimes says \(rR\) captures \(G_2\)-equivariant linear dynamics, while §5.1 explicitly allows \(R\) to be an arbitrary orthogonal map that need not be \(G_2\)-equivariant. Thus the paper conflates “\(G_2\)-structured” with “\(G_2\)-equivariant.”
**Suggested fix:** Introduce two categories: (i) strictly \(G_2\)-equivariant affine maps, where \(R\in\operatorname{Comm}(G_2)\) and \(b=0\); and (ii) \(G_2\)-structured affine maps on a \(G_2\)-module, where \(R\in O(V)\) and \(b\) may break symmetry. Do not attribute the general-\(R\) rate statement to Schur.

### Issue 9: Theorem 9.4(c) gives the wrong general closed form at \(\theta=\pi/2\)
**Location:** §5.3, Theorem 9.4(c), lines 930–936
**Severity:** Critical
**Description:** The displayed formulas for general \(b_A,b_B\) are wrong. At \(\theta=\pi/2\), \(r_A=r_B=r\), \(R_A=R_B=R\), the fixed-point equations are
\[
\hat x+rR\hat y=b_A,\qquad -rR\hat x+\,\hat y=b_B.
\]
Solving gives
\[
\hat x=(I+r^2R^2)^{-1}(b_A-rRb_B),
\]
\[
\hat y=(I+r^2R^2)^{-1}(rRb_A+b_B).
\]
The manuscript instead inserts an extra \((I-rR)\) multiplying \(b_A\) in \(\hat x\) and an extra \((I+rR)\) multiplying \(b_B\) in \(\hat y\). Those extra terms vanish only in the special symmetric-bias simplification after combining \(b_A=b_B=b\).
**Suggested fix:** Replace the general formulas with the two formulas above. Then retain the special case \(b_A=b_B=b\), where the manuscript's simplified \(\hat x=(I-rR)(I+r^2R^2)^{-1}b\) and \(\hat y=(I+rR)(I+r^2R^2)^{-1}b\) are correct because \(R\) commutes with polynomials in \(R\).

### Issue 10: The proof of Theorem 9.4(c) is not a proof
**Location:** §5.3, proof of Theorem 9.4(c), lines 949–963
**Severity:** Critical
**Description:** The proof contains placeholder algebra (“\(\cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\)”) and the \(s,d\) equations have sign errors. With \(s=\hat x+\\hat y\) and \(d=\hat x-\hat y\), the correct equations are
\[
s-rRd=b_A+b_B,
\qquad d+rRs=b_A-b_B.
\]
Thus
\[
(I+r^2R^2)s=(I+rR)b_A+(I-rR)b_B,
\]
not the expression printed in the manuscript. The manuscript's proof does not derive the stated formula and cannot be accepted as a mathematical proof.
**Suggested fix:** Rewrite the proof from the two fixed-point equations, either by block elimination or by the corrected \(s,d\) system above. Remove all placeholder algebra and do not cite numerical verification as a substitute for the derivation.

### Issue 11: The linear-response expansion has the wrong signs
**Location:** §5.4, lines 985–993; used again in Lemma 5.5.1 lines 1046–1053
**Severity:** Critical
**Description:** The Neumann expansion formula itself is correct, but it is applied with the wrong signs. Since
\[
M'(0)=\begin{pmatrix}0&r_A R_A\\-r_B R_B&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
M(\theta)^{-1}=M(0)^{-1}-\theta M(0)^{-1}M'(0)M(0)^{-1}+O(\theta^2),
\]
the correct first-order terms are
\[
\hat x(\theta)=x_A^*-	heta r_A(I-r_AR_A)^{-1}R_Ay_B^*+O(\theta^2),
\]
\[
\hat y(\theta)=y_B^*+\theta r_B(I-r_BR_B)^{-1}R_Bx_A^*+O(\theta^2).
\]
The manuscript prints the opposite signs. This error propagates into the small-\(\theta\) coherence discussion.
**Suggested fix:** Correct the signs and redo Lemma 5.5.1 and Proposition 9.5 from the corrected perturbation formula.

### Issue 12: Lemma 5.5.1 is not rigorous and is not true under the paper's allowed hypotheses
**Location:** §5.5, Lemma 5.5.1 and proof sketch, lines 1032–1059
**Severity:** Critical
**Description:** The lemma asserts \(d\mathcal C_1/d\theta<0\) generically under opposed biases, but the proof only says the sign is “determined by spectral interaction” and then asserts a “definite sign” for generic random orthogonal \(R_A\). That is not a mathematical argument: genericity may imply nonzero, not a prescribed negative sign. The lemma also allows all orthogonal \(R_A,R_B\) described as generic but gives no open dense set, no probability statement, and no explicit inequality. Moreover, under allowed non-generic hypotheses such as \(R_A=R_B=I\), opposed biases remain exactly aligned with their own references for all \(\theta\) in the one-dimensional invariant subspace, so \(\mathcal C_{\min}\) does not strictly decrease.
**Suggested fix:** Replace the lemma by an explicit derivative formula for \(d\mathcal C_i/d\theta\) in terms of \(R_A,R_B,b_A,b_B\). Then state sign conclusions only under explicit checkable inequalities. If the result is probabilistic for random Haar orthogonal matrices, state and prove a probabilistic theorem or label it numerical evidence.

### Issue 13: Proposition 9.5(a) is unproved and false as a universal statement
**Location:** §5.6, Proposition 9.5(a), lines 1068–1099
**Severity:** Critical
**Description:** The proposition says a nonempty min-coherence improvement region exists for a \(G_2\)-structured dyad with the stated hypotheses. That is false without additional assumptions. For example, if \(R_A=R_B=I\), each decoupled fixed point is exactly aligned with its own bias, so \(\mathcal C_1(0)=\mathcal C_2(0)=1\), and no improvement above 1 is possible. The proof also invokes Lemma 5.5.1 “at small \(\varphi>0\),” but Lemma 5.5.1 is about opposed biases \(\varphi=\pi\), not nearly aligned biases. This is a direct mismatch.
**Suggested fix:** Recast (a) as an existence statement for at least one explicitly specified geometry, or impose hypotheses ensuring \(\mathcal C_{\min}(0)<1\) and positive first-order derivatives for both observers. Then prove openness by continuity. Do not claim existence for every allowed dyad.

### Issue 14: Proposition 9.5(b) relies on unproved global monotonicity and an unjustified equality
**Location:** §5.6, proof of Proposition 9.5(b), lines 1101–1108; §6.4 lines 1263–1274
**Severity:** Critical
**Description:** The proof uses a local derivative at \(\theta=0\) plus one numerical grid to claim \(\mathcal C_1(\theta)<\mathcal C_1(0)\) for all \(\theta\in(0,\pi/2]\). That is not a proof. It also concludes \(\mathcal C_1(0)=\mathcal C_{\min}(0)\), but admits this equality only holds in the verification setup. Therefore the claimed global opposed-bias degradation is not established. In fact, the allowed case \(R_A=R_B=I\), \(b_B=-b_A\) gives \(\mathcal C_{\min}(\theta)=\mathcal C_{\min}(0)=1\) for all \(\theta\) as long as the scalar fixed-point components keep their signs, contradicting the strict inequality.
**Suggested fix:** Either weaken the theorem to “in the verified numerical configuration” or add explicit hypotheses and a proof of global monotonicity. Remove downstream claims that opposed biases are guaranteed to produce strict degradation for any \(\theta>0\).

### Issue 15: Proposition 9.5(c) does not prove generic non-universality
**Location:** §5.6, proof of Proposition 9.5(c), lines 1110–1117; §7.2 lines 1314–1332
**Severity:** Major
**Description:** Saying the optimality equation is “transcendental” does not prove that no closed form exists and does not prove that \(\theta^*\ne\pi/4\) generically. Numerical examples can disprove the claim that \(\pi/4\) is universally optimal, but they do not establish a generic theorem.
**Suggested fix:** State the modest result that at least one verified configuration has \(\theta^*\ne\pi/4\), or prove genericity by showing that the condition for \(\pi/4\) to be critical is a nontrivial algebraic/analytic constraint on \((R_A,R_B,b_A,b_B)\) and hence has empty interior or measure zero.

### Issue 16: Numerical verification is repeatedly used as a proof substitute
**Location:** §4.2 lines 688–692; §5.3 lines 962–963; §5.6 lines 1103–1117; Appendix V throughout
**Severity:** Major
**Description:** Numerical verification is useful sanity checking, but finite grids at high precision do not prove universal mathematical statements. The manuscript sometimes uses verification to patch missing algebra, global monotonicity, or genericity claims. This is especially damaging in Proposition 9.5, where the numerical setup is one geometry but the proposition is stated as a theorem about a class of dyads.
**Suggested fix:** Clearly separate theorem proofs from numerical evidence. Use numerical evidence only after an analytic proof, or downgrade the claim to a computational observation for the tested parameter set.

### Issue 17: The coherence functional's range is misstated
**Location:** §5.5 lines 1009–1020; §6.1 line 1201
**Severity:** Minor
**Description:** The functional \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{ref}}(v)=\langle v/\|v\|,e_{\mathrm{ref}}\rangle\) takes values in \([-1,1]\), not \([0,1]\), unless an absolute value, positive-part convention, or acute-angle restriction is imposed. Later the paper states \(\mathcal C_{\min}\in[0,1]\).
**Suggested fix:** Either define coherence as \(|\langle v/\|v\|,e_{\mathrm{ref}}\rangle|\), or state the domain restrictions that ensure nonnegative alignment. Otherwise use \([-1,1]\).

### Issue 18: The paper's “rate channel locked by Schur” slogan is too broad
**Location:** Abstract lines 36–45; §4.4 lines 747–778; §10.8 lines 1644–1652
**Severity:** Major
**Description:** Schur's lemma locks only strictly \(G_2\)-equivariant linear endomorphisms of the irreducible adjoint module to scalars. The affine section permits arbitrary orthogonal \(R_A,R_B\), which are generally not \(G_2\)-equivariant. The rate still equals \(\max(r_A,r_B)\) for those maps because orthogonal factors do not change singular values, not because of Schur. The current slogan blurs two different mechanisms.
**Suggested fix:** Say: “In the strictly \(G_2\)-equivariant linear category, Schur forces scalar rates; in the broader \(G_2\)-structured orthogonal-affine category used in §5, rate preservation follows from orthogonal invariance of the operator norm.”

## Issues NOT raised

- The spectral decomposition of \(\Psi_{\mathrm{sym}}\) into symmetric and antisymmetric subspaces is sound under the canonical Hilbert direct-sum setup and \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\).
- The corrected proof of Theorem 9.2 is short and valid once written using orthogonal invariance of singular values.
- Real Schur's lemma is being used correctly for the specific 14-dimensional adjoint representation: \(\operatorname{End}_{G_2}(V^{14})=\mathbb R\) is true.
- Lemma 3.3.1's one-parameter group and diagonal \(G_2\)-equivariance properties are correct after making the canonical identification of the two copies of \(V^{14}\) explicit.
- The existence and uniqueness part of Theorem 9.4 is salvageable: for orthogonal \(R_A,R_B\), the linear part has norm \(\max(r_A,r_B)<1\), so Banach's theorem applies.
- The special symmetric-bias formula \(\hat x=(I-rR)(I+r^2R^2)^{-1}b\), \(\hat y=(I+rR)(I+r^2R^2)^{-1}b\) at \(\theta=\pi/2\), \(r_A=r_B\), \(R_A=R_B\), \(b_A=b_B\), is correct even though the more general formula printed above it is not.
- The affine equivariance observation that a nonzero bias cannot be fixed by the full adjoint \(G_2\)-action is correct.

## Overall assessment

The paper has a viable negative rate-channel core, but the proof as written is not yet publication-ready. Theorem 9.1 is basically correct after adding positivity and Hilbert-space assumptions. Theorem 9.2 is correct but proved incorrectly; the right proof is a one-line singular-value invariance argument. Corollary 9.3 is correct for the adjoint representation but should not be stated more broadly than justified.

The affine section is where the manuscript breaks. Theorem 9.4 contains an incorrect general closed form, its proof has placeholder algebra, and the linear-response expansion has the signs reversed. Those are not cosmetic errors: Lemma 5.5.1 and Proposition 9.5 depend on the wrong perturbation formula. Proposition 9.5, as stated, is neither proved nor true under the stated hypotheses; simple allowed cases such as \(R_A=R_B=I\) contradict the claimed strict min-coherence improvement/degradation behavior.

Priority fixes: first correct the ambient norm assumptions and the proof of Theorem 9.2; second rewrite Theorem 9.4(c) and §5.4 from scratch; third replace Lemma 5.5.1 and Proposition 9.5 with derivative formulas plus explicitly stated sign hypotheses, or downgrade them to numerical observations for specified geometries. Until those changes are made, the manuscript's central affine-channel claims should not be treated as theorems.
