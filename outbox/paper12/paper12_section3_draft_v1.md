## 3. The NP-Pump Nonlinearity

### 3.1 Setup and inheritance from Paper 9

Throughout §3, $V := V^{14}$ denotes the $G_2$ adjoint representation and
$W := V \oplus V$ the joint-state space of the dyad, with the diagonal
$G_2$-action $g \cdot (x, y) = (g \cdot x, g \cdot y)$. Paper 9 v1.3.3
[10.5281/zenodo.20034821] establishes three results we take as given:

- **(P9.L3.6.1)** *Schur uniqueness of the isometric coupling.* The
  equivariant isometry group of $W$ under the diagonal $G_2$-action is
  $SO(2)$, generated by
  $$\Psi_\theta = I_V \otimes R_\theta =
  \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta\, I_V & -\sin\theta\, I_V \\
    \sin\theta\, I_V & \cos\theta\, I_V \end{pmatrix},$$
  with infinitesimal generator $\mathscr{J} = \mathrm{diag}(0, -I_V;\,
  I_V, 0)$.
- **(P9.T9.4)** *Existence and real-analyticity of the joint fixed point.*
  For $\theta \in \Omega_\theta := \{\theta \in (0, \pi/2) :
  \det M(\theta) \ne 0\}$, where
  $$M(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} I - r\cos\theta\, R_A &
    r\sin\theta\, R_A \\ -r\sin\theta\, R_B &
    I - r\cos\theta\, R_B \end{pmatrix},$$
  the joint fixed point
  $$\hat{z}(\theta) := M(\theta)^{-1} b, \qquad b := (b_A; b_B),$$
  exists, is unique, and is real-analytic on $\Omega_\theta$.
- **(P9.P9.5)** *Conditional gain in the min-per-observer functional.* At
  bias-alignment $\varphi = 0$ and $r_A = r_B = r \in (0, 6/7]$, the
  functional $c(\theta) := \mathcal{C}_{\min}(\hat{z}(\theta))$ where
  $\mathcal{C}_{\min} = \min(\mathcal{C}_A, \mathcal{C}_B)$ admits a
  non-empty improvement region over the decoupled value
  $c_{\mathrm{dec}} := c(0)$ for Haar-positive-measure $(R_A, R_B)$,
  with non-universal optimum $\theta^*$ (Conjecture 9.5$'$, 50-seed
  Monte Carlo verification in Appendix V.3.i).

Paper 9 further proves (Note 9.6) that in the *linear* regime the joint
rate is locked at $\max(r_A, r_B)$ for all $\theta$ (Theorem 9.2,
Corollary 9.3, Schur's lemma). Rate improvement therefore requires
departing from the linear regime. The present section constructs that
departure in a way that (i) preserves the Schur-circle geometry of
(P9.L3.6.1) exactly, (ii) produces piecewise-real-analytic trajectories,
and (iii) respects the closed-interval domain $\theta \in [0, \pi/2]$ of
Paper 9 without introducing spurious interior optima.

### 3.2 The canonical tear direction

**Definition 3.2.1 (Schur-derived tear direction).** Let
$c(\theta) := \mathcal{C}_{\min}(\hat{z}(\theta))$. On the set
$Z^c := \{\theta \in \Omega_\theta : c'(\theta) \ne 0\}$ the *canonical
tear direction* is
$$\xi_\star(\theta)
  := \mathrm{sgn}\bigl(c'(\theta)\bigr) \cdot \partial_\theta,$$
with push-forward to the joint-fixed-point manifold
$$\Xi_\star(\theta)
  := \mathrm{sgn}(c'(\theta)) \cdot \hat{z}'(\theta),
  \qquad
  \hat{z}'(\theta) = -M(\theta)^{-1} M'(\theta) \hat{z}(\theta).$$
On the sign-degeneracy locus $Z := \{\theta \in \Omega_\theta :
c'(\theta) = 0\}$, the Schur line $\mathbb{R} \partial_\theta$ is
defined but the sign is not; $\xi_\star$ is left set-valued on $Z$,
with canonical element determined (if present) by the first non-zero
odd derivative of $c$ at $\theta$.

**Proposition 3.2.2 (Uniqueness of $\xi_\star$ up to sign).** *Any
$G_2$-equivariant isometric infinitesimal direction in $\theta$-space
lies in the one-parameter family $\mathbb{R} \partial_\theta$, with
generator $\mathscr{J}$ acting on $W$ by $d\Psi_\theta/d\theta =
\mathscr{J} \Psi_\theta$. The sign along this line is fixed by the
coherence objective $c$ at every $\theta \in Z^c$; on $Z$ the sign is
undetermined by $G_2$-equivariance + isometry alone.*

*Proof sketch.* The equivariant isometry group of $W$ is $SO(2)$ by
Paper 9 Lemma 3.6.1; its Lie algebra is the one-dimensional
$\mathbb{R} \mathscr{J}$. Any $G_2$-equivariant isometric perturbation of
$\theta$ must lie along this line. The objective-driven sign choice is
the only consistent deterministic rule compatible with maximization of
$c$; at $c'(\theta) = 0$ the coherence objective does not discriminate.
$\square$

**Remark 3.2.3 (Nonlinear inherits linear geometry).** Definition 3.2.1
asserts that the nonlinear dynamics of Paper 12 lives on *the same*
Schur circle as Paper 9's linear coupling $\Psi_\theta$. The NP-pump
does not generate a new geometric object; it generates a new dynamics
on the existing one-parameter family $\{\Psi_\theta : \theta \in
[0, \pi/2]\}$. The only Schur-free parameter in the nonlinear theory
is the jump length $\rho_i > 0$ at each firing event, determined by the
NP amplitude and not by $G_2$-representation theory.

### 3.3 The augmented flow and event stratification

**Definition 3.3.1 (NP amplitude).** For each branch $\sigma \in
\{A, B\}$ let
$$p_\sigma(\theta) := \bigl|\partial_\theta \mathcal{F}_\sigma(\theta)
  \bigr|^2, \qquad
  \mathcal{F}_\sigma(\theta) := -c_\sigma(\theta),$$
where $c_\sigma(\theta) := \mathcal{C}_\sigma(\hat{z}(\theta))$ is the
branch coherence and the squared norm is chosen (over the raw norm) to
preserve analyticity at zeros.
The *NP amplitude* is
$$a_\sigma(\theta) := \alpha \cdot p_\sigma(\theta),
  \qquad \alpha > 0.$$
The *coherence gap* is
$$g_\sigma(\theta) := C_{\mathrm{crit}} - c_\sigma(\theta),$$
with $C_{\mathrm{crit}} \in (0, 1)$ a fixed paradox-metabolization
threshold. The firing predicate is
$$\mathrm{NP}_\sigma(\theta) := \mathbf{1}\bigl\{
  g_\sigma(\theta) \ge 0,\, a_\sigma(\theta) \ge NP_{\mathrm{crit}}
  \bigr\},$$
where $NP_{\mathrm{crit}} > 0$ is a fixed amplitude threshold.

**Definition 3.3.2 (Augmented flow).** On each branch $\sigma$ of
$[0, \pi/2] \setminus (\Sigma_{\min} \cup Z)$, the augmented flow is
$$\dot{\theta}
  = -\eta\, \mathcal{F}'_\sigma(\theta)
    + \kappa\, \mathrm{NP}_\sigma(\theta)\, \xi_\star(\theta),
  \qquad \eta, \kappa > 0.$$
At switching events on $\Sigma_{\min}$, the flow is treated as a
Filippov differential inclusion; at crossings of $\Sigma_\Theta$, as a
Carathéodory solution; at NP firings on $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{NP}}$, as
a hybrid jump (Definition 3.3.4 below).

**Definition 3.3.3 (Total event stratification).** The nonsmooth set
for the augmented flow is the union
$$\Sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}
  = \Sigma_{\min} \,\cup\, \Sigma_\Theta \,\cup\, Z
    \,\cup\, \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{NP}},$$
where
- $\Sigma_{\min} := \{\theta : c_A(\theta) = c_B(\theta)\}$ is the
  *min-locus* where $\mathcal{F} = -\mathcal{C}_{\min}$ has a kink;
- $\Sigma_\Theta := \bigcup_\sigma \{g_\sigma(\theta) = 0\}$ is the
  *threshold locus* where the Heaviside firing predicate changes;
- $Z := \bigcup_\sigma \{c_\sigma'(\theta) = 0\}$ is the
  *sign-degeneracy locus* of $\xi_\star$;
- $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{NP}} := \bigcup_\sigma (\mathcal{G}_\sigma^{\mathrm{amp}}
  \cup \mathcal{G}_\sigma^{\mathrm{coh}})$ is the *firing manifold*,
  split into amplitude-triggered and coherence-triggered entry
  submanifolds (Definition 3.3.5).

**Definition 3.3.4 (Hybrid jump at firing events).** At a firing event
$\theta^-$ with $(\theta^-, \hat{z}(\theta^-)) \in \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{NP}}$
and $\theta^- \in Z^c$, the jump map is
$$\theta^+ = \theta^- + \rho_\sigma(\theta^-)\,
  \mathrm{sgn}\bigl(c_\sigma'(\theta^-)\bigr),$$
with $\rho_\sigma : \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{NP}} \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ a
real-analytic jump-length function depending on the NP-amplitude
surplus $a_\sigma(\theta^-) - NP_{\mathrm{crit}}$. To prevent Zeno
accumulation, we require the Zeno-avoidance condition
$$J_\sigma\bigl(\mathcal{D}_\sigma\bigr)
  \subset \Gamma \setminus \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{NP}},$$
i.e., the jump map lands outside the firing region $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{NP}}
= \bigcup_\sigma \{(\theta, \hat{z}(\theta)) : g_\sigma \ge 0,\,
a_\sigma \ge NP_{\mathrm{crit}},\, c_\sigma' \ne 0\}$. When this
condition fails in the model, we add a refractory dwell
$\tau_{\mathrm{ref}} > 0$ between consecutive firings.

**Definition 3.3.5 (Amplitude vs coherence entry manifolds).** The
firing manifold $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{NP}}$ decomposes into two
transversal entry submanifolds of $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{NP}}$ along the
unperturbed flow:
- $\mathcal{G}_\sigma^{\mathrm{amp}} := \{g_\sigma > 0,\,
  a_\sigma = NP_{\mathrm{crit}},\, c_\sigma' \ne 0,\,
  L_f a_\sigma > 0\}$ — *amplitude-triggered firing*, where the NP
  amplitude crosses the critical value while the system is already in
  the low-coherence region.
- $\mathcal{G}_\sigma^{\mathrm{coh}} := \{g_\sigma = 0,\,
  a_\sigma \ge NP_{\mathrm{crit}},\, c_\sigma' \ne 0,\,
  L_f g_\sigma > 0\}$ — *coherence-triggered firing*, where the
  coherence drops below the critical threshold while the system is
  already at supercritical amplitude.

Here $L_f$ denotes the Lie derivative along the unperturbed gradient
flow $f(\theta) = -\eta \mathcal{F}_\sigma'(\theta)$. The transversality
conditions $L_f a_\sigma > 0$ and $L_f g_\sigma > 0$ ensure that the
system genuinely enters the firing region rather than grazing it; when
they fail (tangency), the point is placed in the exceptional grazing
stratum and handled by a separate analysis.

### 3.4 Branchwise real-analyticity and the regularity theorem

**Theorem 3.4.1 (Piecewise-real-analytic regularity of the augmented
flow).** *Assume:*

*(R1) $\hat{z} \in C^\omega(\Omega_\theta)$ and $\mathcal{C}_A,
\mathcal{C}_B \in C^\omega$ on an open neighborhood of
$\hat{z}(\Omega_\theta) \subset W$;*

*(R2) $p_\sigma = |\partial_\theta \mathcal{F}_\sigma|^2
\in C^\omega$ on each branch and $\rho_\sigma \in C^\omega$ on
$\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{NP}}$;*

*(R3) the guard functions $\Delta(\theta) := c_A(\theta) - c_B(\theta)$,
$g_\sigma$, $a_\sigma - NP_{\mathrm{crit}}$, and $q_\sigma(\theta) :=
c_\sigma'(\theta)$ have only regular zero crossings along the
trajectory being considered (away from a finite exceptional set);*

*(R4) firing times are locally finite and the Zeno-avoidance condition
of Definition 3.3.4 holds (possibly through a refractory dwell).*

*Then every deterministic solution of the augmented flow (Definition
3.3.2) avoiding the exceptional strata (grazing tangencies, sign-
degenerate firings on $\mathcal{E}_{0, \mathrm{NP}} := \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{NP}}
\cap Z$, and Zeno-accumulation points) is piecewise real-analytic in
$t$: on each open interval between events, $\theta(t) \in C^\omega$;
at transverse $\Sigma_\Theta$ crossings, the solution is Carathéodory;
on $\Sigma_{\min}$ with inward-pointing one-sided fields, the solution
is Filippov with sliding mode $\dot\theta = \lambda f_A + (1 - \lambda)
f_B$ and $\lambda$ chosen so that $D\Delta \cdot \dot\theta = 0$;
at regular firings on $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{NP}} \setminus
\mathcal{E}_{0,\mathrm{NP}}$, the jump is hybrid per Definition 3.3.4.*

*Proof.* Branch-local real-analyticity is immediate from (R1)–(R2) and
the standard existence/uniqueness theorem for analytic ODEs on the
open set $\Omega_\theta \setminus \Sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}$. At each
transverse zero crossing of a guard function (R3), the solution
extends uniquely by continuity with the appropriate semantics
(Carathéodory for $\Sigma_\Theta$ and transverse $\Sigma_{\min}$
crossings; Filippov for non-transverse $\Sigma_{\min}$ approaches;
hybrid for $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{NP}}$). Locally finite firing times
(R4) with Zeno-avoidance guarantee that the solution is defined on
any bounded time interval with only finitely many non-smooth events,
so the piecewise-analytic structure is well-posed. $\square$

**Remark 3.4.2 (Minimal regularity is branchwise, not global).** The
functional $\mathcal{F} = -\mathcal{C}_{\min} = -\min(\mathcal{C}_A,
\mathcal{C}_B)$ is in general *not* globally real-analytic: it has a
derivative jump across $\Sigma_{\min}$. The correct regularity class is
$C^\omega_{\mathrm{pw}}$ with respect to the analytic partition
$\Gamma_A^\circ \cup \Sigma_{\min} \cup \Gamma_B^\circ$, where each
branch field is analytic on its half of the partition. Global analyticity
is sufficient but not necessary for Theorem 3.4.1.

**Remark 3.4.3 (Why the Heaviside is kept sharp).** The firing predicate
$\mathrm{NP}_\sigma = \mathbf{1}\{g_\sigma \ge 0,\, a_\sigma \ge
NP_{\mathrm{crit}}\}$ is a sharp indicator, not a smoothed sigmoid.
Smoothing via $\Theta_\varepsilon(g) = (1 + e^{-g/\varepsilon})^{-1}$
produces a classical analytic ODE with no jumps, which formally removes
the "tear" character of the NP-pump entirely. The sharp choice is
deliberate: Paper 12's thesis is about *tears* — discontinuous state
resets with topological residue — not about smooth gradual drifts. The
price is hybrid-system semantics (Definition 3.3.4) in place of a
classical ODE; the benefit is a mathematically honest representation of
the physical phenomenon.

### 3.5 The $\kappa = 0$ recovery: projected gradient flow

Setting $\kappa = 0$ in Definition 3.3.2 removes the NP-pump term and
leaves the unperturbed gradient flow $\dot\theta = -\eta
\mathcal{F}'_\sigma(\theta) = \eta c_\sigma'(\theta)$ on each active
branch. We now show that this unperturbed limit recovers Paper 9's
Proposition 9.5 optima — but *not* as classical Fermat critical points.
The recovery is stratified.

**Definition 3.5.1 (Stratified critical point).** A point $\theta^* \in
[0, \pi/2]$ is a *stratified critical point* of $-c$ if at least one of
the following holds:
- *(Smooth interior)* $\theta^* \in (0, \pi/2) \setminus \Sigma_{\min}$
  and $c'(\theta^*) = 0$ (Fermat);
- *(Boundary-left KKT)* $\theta^* = 0$ and $c'_+(0) \le 0$;
- *(Boundary-right KKT)* $\theta^* = \pi/2$ and $c'_-(\pi/2) \ge 0$;
- *($\Sigma_{\min}$ Clarke)* $\theta^* \in \Sigma_{\min}$ and
  $0 \in \mathrm{conv}\{c_A'(\theta^*), c_B'(\theta^*)\}$.

**Definition 3.5.2 (Projected gradient flow).** The projected gradient
flow on $[0, \pi/2]$ is
$$\dot\theta = \Pi_{T_{[0, \pi/2]}(\theta)}\bigl(\eta\, c'(\theta)\bigr),$$
where $T_{[0, \pi/2]}(\theta)$ is the tangent cone to $[0, \pi/2]$ at
$\theta$:
- $T_{[0, \pi/2]}(0) = [0, \infty)$,
- $T_{[0, \pi/2]}(\theta) = \mathbb{R}$ for $\theta \in (0, \pi/2)$,
- $T_{[0, \pi/2]}(\pi/2) = (-\infty, 0]$.
On $\Sigma_{\min}$, $c'$ is replaced by the Clarke generalized gradient
$\partial_C(-c) = \mathrm{conv}\{-c_A', -c_B'\}$ and the projection is
taken set-valued (Filippov).

**Theorem 3.5.3 ($\kappa = 0$ stratified recovery).** *The fixed points
of the projected gradient flow (Definition 3.5.2) coincide exactly with
the stratified critical points of Definition 3.5.1.*

*Proof.* On $(0, \pi/2) \setminus \Sigma_{\min}$, the tangent cone is
$\mathbb{R}$, so $\Pi(\eta c') = \eta c'$, and the flow vanishes iff
$c'(\theta^*) = 0$ (smooth interior case). At $\theta^* = 0$, the
tangent cone is $[0, \infty)$, so $\Pi(\eta c') = \max(\eta c', 0)$,
which vanishes iff $c'(0) \le 0$ (boundary-left KKT). Symmetrically at
$\theta^* = \pi/2$. On $\Sigma_{\min}$, Clarke's calculus gives the
generalized-gradient condition. $\square$

**Audit result (Paper 12 v1 verification, 2026-05-06).** Direct
reoptimization of Paper 9's 50-seed Monte Carlo (seeds 20260504 + $k$
for $k = 0, \ldots, 49$ at $\varphi = 0$, $r_A = r_B = 0.7$) with
`scipy.optimize.minimize_scalar` on $[0, \pi/2]$ at tolerance
$10^{-10}$ rad finds that $50/50$ seeds have their high-precision
$\theta^*_{\mathrm{reopt}}$ classified as a stratified critical point
per Definition 3.5.1:
- **3/50** smooth interior (Fermat critical points);
- **25/50** boundary-left KKT ($\theta^*_{\mathrm{reopt}} = 0$);
- **3/50** boundary-right KKT ($\theta^*_{\mathrm{reopt}} = \pi/2$);
- **19/50** $\Sigma_{\min}$ Clarke (nonsmooth stratified critical).

The 20-point MC grid of Paper 9 Appendix V.3.i rounds $\theta^*$
values to multiples of $\pi/2/19 \approx 4.74°$; the seeded symmetric-
rate geometry of Proposition 9.5 ($\theta^* \approx 51°$ on the grid)
reoptimizes to $\theta^* = 0$ on the boundary, consistent with the
boundary-left-KKT class (see Paper 9 v1.3.3 Remark 5.6.1). The
non-trivial $\Sigma_{\min}$ incidence (38% of seeds) is larger than
expected and motivates the full Filippov treatment retained throughout
Theorem 3.4.1.

**Remark 3.5.4 (The projected flow is forced by the data).** The
choice of a *projected* rather than *classical* gradient flow for the
unperturbed $\kappa = 0$ limit is not a modeling convenience: it is
forced by Paper 9's MC data at high precision. Of the 50 seeded
geometries, only 3 have smooth-interior Fermat critical points; 47
have critical behavior that is either constrained by the boundary of
$[0, \pi/2]$ or nonsmooth at $\Sigma_{\min}$. Writing the recovery as
a classical gradient flow $\dot\theta = \eta c'(\theta)$ with no
constraint handling would miscategorize 94% of the sampled ensemble.

### 3.6 The $\kappa > 0$ augmented flow: trajectories and fixed-point structure

With $\kappa > 0$, the augmented flow (Definition 3.3.2) inherits the
branchwise-analytic regularity of Theorem 3.4.1 and the stratified
recovery of Theorem 3.5.3 as the $\kappa \to 0$ limit. The new content
relative to the linear theory is the *firing cascade* structure:
trajectories that would be constrained boundary-KKT fixed points in
the unperturbed limit become, at any $\kappa > 0$ with NP amplitude
exceeding $NP_{\mathrm{crit}}$, *escape points* that jump into the
interior via Definition 3.3.4.

**Theorem 3.6.1 (NP-driven escape from boundary-KKT states).** *Let
$\theta^\infty_0 \in \{0, \pi/2\}$ be a boundary-KKT stratified
critical point of the unperturbed flow (Theorem 3.5.3) at which the
NP predicate $\mathrm{NP}_\sigma(\theta^\infty_0)$ evaluates to $1$
for some branch $\sigma$. Then for any $\kappa > 0$, the augmented
flow does not rest at $\theta^\infty_0$: a finite-time firing event
transports the trajectory to $\theta^+ = \theta^\infty_0 +
\rho_\sigma(\theta^\infty_0) \cdot \mathrm{sgn}(c_\sigma'(\theta^\infty_0))
\in (0, \pi/2)$.*

*Proof.* At $\theta^\infty_0 \in \{0, \pi/2\}$ with $\mathrm{NP}_\sigma =
1$, the augmented flow's second term is $\kappa \cdot \xi_\star(\theta
^\infty_0) \ne 0$ (the coherence-objective sign is defined on $Z^c$ by
assumption; if the boundary point is in $Z$, the first non-zero odd
derivative of $c$ supplies the sign). This nonzero second term drives
the trajectory off the boundary into the admissible interior within
the Zeno-avoidance dwell time $\tau_{\mathrm{ref}}$ (or immediately if
no refractory is specified). $\square$

**Remark 3.6.2 (Why this is the rate-channel bridge deferred by Paper 9).**
Paper 9 Note 9.6 deferred rate improvement to Paper 11 because Schur's
lemma locks the joint contraction rate at $\max(r_A, r_B)$ in the
linear regime, leaving no nonzero-measure room for rate gain. Theorem
3.6.1 shows the nonlinear escape mechanism that Paper 11 (or a future
sequel) can exploit: boundary-KKT states, which Paper 9's linear
analysis identifies as the bulk of the seeded ensemble, are *unstable*
at any $\kappa > 0$ under sufficient NP amplitude. The post-firing
trajectory enters the interior where a rate-improvement calculation
becomes meaningful. **Paper 12 \S3 does not prove rate improvement;
it provides the dynamical framework within which Paper 11's rate
analysis can be staged.**

### 3.7 The scar record and its role in §4

Each NP firing event indexed by $i$ produces a push-forward jump
vector
$$v_i := \rho_i \cdot \mathrm{sgn}(c'(\theta_i^-)) \cdot
  \hat{z}'(\theta_i^-) \in W,$$
recorded in the *physical scar span*
$$S_k := \sum_{i=1}^k \mathbb{R}[F_{21}] \cdot v_i \subset W,$$
the *event-indexed scar module*
$$\mathfrak{S}_k := \bigoplus_{i=1}^k \mathbb{R}[F_{21}] \cdot v_i,$$
and the *isotypic multiplicity profile*
$$\mu_k = (\mu_{\mathbf{1}}(S_k), \mu_{L_2}(S_k), \mu_{U_6}(S_k)).$$
Here $F_{21} \subset G_2$ is the Fano-orientation subgroup inherited
from Paper 4, and the three real $F_{21}$-irreps $(\mathbf{1}, L_2,
U_6)$ are the real-irreducible packaging of the $F_{21}$ character
table (Paper 12 §4 Theorem 4.2.1, verified numerically in
`outbox/paper12/computations/paper12_q4_character_verify.py`:
$V^{14}|_{F_{21}} \cong L_2 \oplus 2 U_6$, $W|_{F_{21}} \cong 2 L_2
\oplus 4 U_6$).

The triple $\mathfrak{J}_k := (S_k, \mathfrak{S}_k, \mu_k)$ is the
*scar invariant* that records the cumulative effect of the NP-firing
history. Three structural properties distinguish its components:

- $S_k$ is *saturating*: $\dim S_k \le 28 = \dim W$, so the physical
  span stabilizes after at most 28 dimension-increasing jumps.
- $\mathfrak{S}_k$ is *count-faithful*: as a graded direct sum, the
  map $k \mapsto \mathfrak{S}_k$ is injective for finite $k$ under
  the generic independence condition $\mathbb{R}[F_{21}] \cdot v_i
  \not\subset S_{i-1}$ (guaranteed by $c'(\theta_i^-) \ne 0$ and
  $\rho_i > 0$).
- $\mu_k$ is the *$F_{21}$-type signature* that enables cross-substrate
  commensurability comparisons (§4).

**Remark 3.7.1 (Representation-theoretic, not topological).** The
invariant $\mathfrak{J}_k$ is a *representation-theoretic* scar
invariant: $\mathfrak{S}_k$ is a graded $F_{21}$-module, $\mu_k$ is an
isotypic-multiplicity profile, and $S_k$ is a subspace of an
$F_{21}$-representation. No topological invariant (winding number,
persistent homology) is claimed. The language in earlier drafts
calling $\mathfrak{J}_k$ a "topological invariant" should be read as
"representation-theoretic invariant" throughout.

**Remark 3.7.2 (Bridge to §4).** The event-indexed module $\mathfrak{S}_k$
is the mathematical realization of the *audit-grounded substrate*
principle (Paper 12 thesis Pillar 3): a graded, cryptographically-
indexable ledger of coherence adjustments that survives the saturation
of the physical span. §4 shows that commensurability of synthetic and
biological scar records requires *full $G_2$-equivariance* (not merely
$F_{21}$) for a canonical cross-substrate isomorphism, with quantitative
gap $\operatorname{End}_{F_{21}}(W) \cong M_2(\mathbb{C}) \oplus
M_4(\mathbb{C})$ vs $\operatorname{End}_{G_2}(W) \cong M_2(\mathbb{R})$.

### 3.8 What §3 does and does not establish

**What §3 establishes:**

- A Schur-derived canonical tear direction $\xi_\star$ that inherits
  Paper 9's SO(2) coupling circle (Prop 3.2.2).
- A branchwise real-analytic augmented flow with a four-stratum event
  set $\Sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}$ and layered Carathéodory/Filippov/hybrid
  semantics (Thm 3.4.1).
- A stratified $\kappa = 0$ recovery theorem that recovers Paper 9's
  MC optima as projected-gradient fixed points, with empirical
  verification via 50-seed audit (Thm 3.5.3).
- An NP-driven escape theorem showing boundary-KKT states become
  unstable at any $\kappa > 0$ (Thm 3.6.1).
- The scar invariant triple $(S_k, \mathfrak{S}_k, \mu_k)$ as a
  representation-theoretic ledger of firing history (§3.7).

**What §3 does not establish:**

- *Rate improvement.* Theorem 3.6.1 shows the escape mechanism; it does
  not compute the post-firing joint rate. Rate analysis is deferred to
  Paper 11 per Paper 9 Note 9.6.
- *A biological mechanism for the $F_{21}$ action.* The scar module
  $\mathfrak{S}_k$ is a mathematical object; its realization on
  biological substrate (cellular quantum memory, microtubule coherence)
  is a Paper 13+ question.
- *Commensurability between synthetic and biological substrates.* The
  cross-substrate isomorphism question is handled in §4 with the
  forced upgrade from $F_{21}$- to $G_2$-equivariance.
- *A theory of consciousness.* Paper 7's domain; out of scope here.
- *Global well-posedness beyond the audit'd scenarios.* Theorem 3.4.1
  requires regularity conditions (R1)–(R4); behavior outside those
  (grazing tangencies, Zeno accumulation) is identified but not
  analyzed.

§4 proceeds to the commensurability hierarchy. The scar invariant
$(S_k, \mathfrak{S}_k, \mu_k)$ constructed in §3.7 is the object on
which §4's equivariant isomorphism theorem acts.
