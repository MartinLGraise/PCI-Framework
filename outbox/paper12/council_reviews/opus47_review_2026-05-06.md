## §3 Issues (NP-pump nonlinearity)

### S3-1. Sign error in NP-jump rule at boundary-KKT states  *(CRITICAL)*

**Where.** Definition 3.3.4 + Theorem 3.6.1.

**Problem.** The jump rule is \(\theta^+ = \theta^- + \rho_\sigma
\,\mathrm{sgn}(c'_\sigma(\theta^-))\). At a boundary-left KKT point
\(\theta^- = 0\), Definition 3.5.1 requires \(c'_+(0) \le 0\), so
\(\mathrm{sgn}(c'(0)) \in \{-1, 0\}\) and \(\theta^+ \le 0\) — *outside*
\([0,\pi/2]\). Mirror failure at \(\pi/2\). The drift term
\(-\eta\mathcal{F}'_\sigma = \eta c'_\sigma\) also points outward. So
both terms of the augmented flow push out of the domain at boundary KKT
points. Theorem 3.6.1 nonetheless asserts \(\theta^+ \in (0,\pi/2)\). Given
that 28/50 audited seeds are boundary-KKT (S3-7), this is load-bearing.

**Fix.** Either (a) replace \(\mathrm{sgn}(c'_\sigma)\) with
\(-\mathrm{sgn}(c'_\sigma)\) so the tear *opposes* the local descent of
\(\mathcal{F}\) at the boundary; (b) project the tear into the tangent cone
of \([0,\pi/2]\) at boundary points; or (c) restrict Theorem 3.6.1 to
non-KKT firings and supply a separate boundary analysis.

### S3-2. Two definitions of \(Z\) disagree  *(MAJOR)*

**Where.** Definition 3.2.1 vs. Definition 3.3.3.

**Problem.** §3.2 defines \(Z := \{\theta : c'(\theta) = 0\}\) for
\(c = \mathcal{C}_{\min}\); §3.3 redefines \(Z := \bigcup_\sigma
\{c'_\sigma = 0\}\). These differ generically: \(c'_{\min}\) zeros are
distinct from branch-derivative zeros except on \(\Sigma_{\min}\), and on
\(\Sigma_{\min}\) the former is multivalued (Clarke).

**Fix.** Use branch-wise \(Z_\sigma := \{c'_\sigma = 0\}\) and
\(Z := Z_A \cup Z_B\). Update Prop 3.2.2 wording to per-branch.

### S3-3. Prop 3.2.2 sign-uniqueness is stipulated, not derived  *(MAJOR)*

**Where.** Proposition 3.2.2 proof sketch.

**Problem.** The proof correctly shows the equivariant-isometry Lie algebra
is one-dimensional; the *line* \(\mathbb{R}\partial_\theta\) is unique. The
claim that "the objective-driven sign choice is the only consistent
deterministic rule" is then false: \(\xi = +\partial_\theta\) is equally
deterministic and \(G_2\)-equivariant. Coherence-ascent is an *additional
design axiom*, not a consequence of equivariance + isometry.

**Fix.** Split: part (i) line uniqueness (Schur); part (ii) sign on \(Z^c\)
fixed by an explicit *coherence-ascent design principle*, stated as an
axiom.

### S3-4. Theorem 3.4.1 (R3) effectively assumes the conclusion  *(MAJOR)*

**Where.** Theorem 3.4.1, hypothesis (R3).

**Problem.** (R3) demands "regular zero crossings of the guard functions
along the trajectory being considered" — which is precisely the piecewise-
analytic structure the theorem aims to conclude. The genuinely nontrivial
content (that analytic \(\hat z\), \(\rho_\sigma\) imply *generic* regular
crossings via a transversality argument) is missing.

**Fix.** Restate (R3) as a transversality condition on parameters
\((R_A, R_B, b_A, b_B, \alpha, NP_{\rm crit}, C_{\rm crit})\) plus a
Sard-style genericity claim (Baire-generic / full-measure parameter set
yields regular crossings). Or rename Theorem 3.4.1 "Conditional Regularity"
and acknowledge the \(\Rightarrow\)-shape.

### S3-5. Filippov sliding-mode well-posedness is incomplete  *(MAJOR)*

**Where.** Theorem 3.4.1 statement, Filippov clause.

**Problem.** The equivalent-control formula \(\dot\theta = \lambda f_A +
(1-\lambda)f_B\) with \(D\Delta\cdot\dot\theta = 0\) requires (i) both
fields point toward \(\Sigma_{\min}\) on entry, (ii) \(D\Delta \ne 0\) at
the contact point, (iii) the solving \(\lambda \in [0,1]\). Only (i) is
mentioned. When (iii) fails, the trajectory must transversally cross
\(\Sigma_{\min}\) (a *crossing* mode), not slide; this third regime is not
in the draft.

**Fix.** Spell out (ii)–(iii); fold (ii) into (R3); add the crossing-mode
case to the theorem.

### S3-6. Theorem 3.5.3 conflates two projections  *(MAJOR)*

**Where.** Definition 3.5.2 + Theorem 3.5.3 proof.

**Problem.** Definition 3.5.2 projects onto the tangent cone of the
*interval* \([0,\pi/2]\). The Clarke generalized-gradient construction at
\(\Sigma_{\min}\) is a different projection. The proof silently combines
them, but does not specify their interaction at endpoints
\(\theta\in\{0,\pi/2\}\cap\Sigma_{\min}\) (which can occur whenever
\(c_A(0)=c_B(0)\)).

**Fix.** Either (a) define a single combined projection onto
\(T_{[0,\pi/2]} \cap T_{\rm Clarke}\) and prove non-emptiness at corners;
or (b) place corner cases in the exceptional stratum and verify the audit
contains none.

### S3-7. 50-seed audit data tension with Conjecture 9.5'  *(MAJOR)*

**Where.** §3.5 audit summary.

**Problem.** 25/50 seeds reoptimize to \(\theta^* = 0\) (boundary-left KKT),
where \(c(\theta^*) = c_{\rm dec}\) — i.e., **no Schur-coupling improvement**
exists for those seeds. Together with 3/50 at \(\theta^* = \pi/2\), 56% of
audited seeds have *no interior improvement* at all. Paper 9 Conjecture
9.5' claims a Haar-positive-measure improvement region; the audit data
sharply restrict this. More urgently for §3.6: Theorem 3.6.1's escape
mechanism transports a boundary-KKT trajectory into the interior, but for
the 28 boundary seeds, *the audit gives no evidence the interior contains
any point with \(c > c_{\rm dec}\)*. Escape into a region of strictly
*lower* coherence is not an improvement.

**Fix.** Add a calculation: for the 28 boundary-KKT seeds, what is
\(\sup_{(0,\pi/2)} c(\theta)\) and is it \(> c(0)\) anywhere? If no, the
escape lands the trajectory in lower-coherence territory, and the framing
of Theorem 3.6.1 needs revision. This audit is required before §3 ships.

### S3-8. Generator notation \(\mathscr{J} = \mathrm{diag}(0,-I_V; I_V, 0)\) is malformed  *(MINOR)*

**Where.** §3.1, (P9.L3.6.1).

**Problem.** \(\mathrm{diag}(\cdot)\) denotes block-diagonal; the intended
object is the off-diagonal antisymmetric block
\(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0 & -I_V \\ I_V & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\).

**Fix.** Use the explicit \(2\times 2\) block form, matching \(\Psi_\theta\).

### S3-9. \(\mathfrak{S}_k\) "count-faithfulness" is mis-stated  *(MINOR)*

**Where.** §3.7, three structural properties.

**Problem.** As an abstract direct sum, \(\mathfrak{S}_k =
\bigoplus_i \mathbb{R}[F_{21}]\cdot v_i\) is *always* count-faithful by
construction. The genericity condition \(\mathbb{R}[F_{21}]\cdot v_i
\not\subset S_{i-1}\) controls injectivity of the *natural map*
\(\mathfrak{S}_k \to S_k \subset W\), not faithfulness of \(\mathfrak{S}_k\).

**Fix.** Distinguish: \(\mathfrak{S}_k\) is count-faithful as an abstract
graded module; the surjection \(\mathfrak{S}_k \twoheadrightarrow S_k\) is
generically injective under the stated condition.

### S3-10. \(F_{21}\)-equivariance of dynamics is silently absent  *(MINOR)*

**Where.** §3.7 \(\to\) §4 transition.

**Problem.** §3 builds dynamics from arbitrary \((R_A, R_B, b_A, b_B)\)
that need not be \(F_{21}\)-related. The \(v_i\) inherit an \(F_{21}\)-action
from \(W\)'s ambient \(G_2\)-structure, but the *dynamics* generating them
is generically not \(F_{21}\)-equivariant.

**Fix.** One sentence in §3.7 making this explicit and clarifying that
\(\mathfrak{J}_k\) extracts the \(F_{21}\)-content of a generically
\(F_{21}\)-asymmetric trajectory.

### S3-11. \(L_f g_\sigma > 0\) coherence-trigger sign needs prose  *(NIT)*

**Where.** Definition 3.3.5, second bullet.

**Problem.** \(L_f g_\sigma > 0\) means \(g\) increases, i.e. \(c\)
decreases. Correct, but the prose ("system already at supercritical
amplitude") could mislead.

**Fix.** Add one clarifying sentence on the direction of guard motion at
entry.

## §4 Issues (commensurability hierarchy)

### S4-1. Theorem 4.4.1 (C2)⇒(C3) has a logical gap  *(MAJOR)*

**Where.** Theorem 4.4.1 proof, second paragraph.

**Problem.** (C2) is a *numerical* condition (multiplicity tuples agree on
ambient operator spaces \(I_{\rm syn}, I_{\rm bio}\)). (C3) requires
agreement of \(\mu_k\) on the *cumulative scar span* \(S_k\) for *every*
event sequence. The proof slips in "an \(F_{21}\)-equivariant identification
of the scar maps (existence of *some* \(\phi_F\), not necessarily
canonical)" — but (C2) does not guarantee any such map exists. As stated,
(C2) and (C3) are logically independent.

**Fix.** Either (a) add an explicit hypothesis "and the scar-encoding maps
are \(F_{21}\)-equivariantly isomorphic" between (C2) and (C3); or
(b) reformulate (C2) as a map-existence condition (existence of an
\(F_{21}\)-equivariant isomorphism aligning scar dynamics) rather than a
profile-match condition.

### S4-2. Cor 4.3.2 dimension count uses wrong connected component  *(MAJOR)*

**Where.** Corollary 4.3.2 proof.

**Problem.** The proof identifies the connected orientation-preserving
subgroup of \(O_{F_{21}}(W) = U(2) \times U(4)\) as
\(SU(2) \times U(4)\), of dimension \(3 + 16 = 19\). But for any
\(U \in U(n)\), \(\det_\mathbb{R}(U) = |\det_\mathbb{C}(U)|^2 = 1\), so all
of \(U(n)\) is orientation-preserving on the underlying real space.
Therefore the connected orientation-preserving subgroup is the full
\(U(2) \times U(4)\), of dimension \(20\). The "19 dimensions" headline
should be the *delta* \(20 - 1 = 19\) (vs. \(SO(2)\)), not the absolute
dimension of the \(F_{21}\)-side group. Also note §4.3 prose says
"reduces ... by 18 real dimensions" while §4.4 cites Cor 4.3.2 as giving
a "19-dim family." Internal contradiction.

**Fix.** Replace \(SU(2)\times U(4)\) with full \(U(2) \times U(4)\) at
\(20\) dim; state "the upgrade reduces the equivariant isometry group by
\(20 - 1 = 19\) real dimensions"; reconcile §4.3's "18" with §4.4's "19" (it
should be 19 throughout).

### S4-3. Cor 4.5.2 "if and only if" overclaims  *(MAJOR)*

**Where.** Corollary 4.5.2.

**Problem.** Stated as a biconditional: canonical \(\phi_{\rm commens}\)
exists *iff* both spaces are absolutely irreducible mult-one. The forward
(sufficiency) direction is proved by Schur; the reverse (necessity) is not
proved anywhere in §4. Counter-tendency: any \(G_2\)-module with isotypic
multiplicities \(\le 1\), all of real type, also admits a unique normalized
equivariant isomorphism to itself.

**Fix.** Downgrade to a *sufficient* condition. Or generalize: "all
\(G_2\)-isotypic multiplicities of \(I_{\rm syn}, I_{\rm bio}\) are
\(\le 1\), agree, and constituents are real type." Don't claim necessity
without proof.

### S4-4. \(\mathbb{R}^7\) prose mis-identifies the action  *(MINOR)*

**Where.** Theorem 4.2.1 proof, "\(s: i \mapsto 2i \bmod 7\)" parenthetical.

**Problem.** Multiplication by \(2\) on \(\mathbb{F}_7^* = \{1,...,6\}\) has
cycle structure \((1,2,4)(3,6,5)\) — *no fixed points* among the seven
imaginary-octonion basis indices. The character \(\mathrm{tr}(r_3) = 1\)
needs *one* fixed point. The correct \(F_{21}\)-action with one
\(\mathbb{Z}_3\)-fixed point is the action on the *Fano plane* (7 lines /
7 points of the projective configuration), where \(\mathbb{Z}_3\) fixes the
unique invariant line. The character calculation lands at the right value;
the prose attribution of \(s\) does not.

**Fix.** Replace the parenthetical: "Acting on the seven points of the
Fano plane with \(s\) of order \(3\) and cycle structure \(1+3+3\); the
fixed point is the unique \(s\)-invariant Fano line, giving
\(\mathrm{tr}(r_3) = 1\)."

### S4-5. Prop 4.5.1 attacks a strawman  *(MINOR)*

**Where.** Proposition 4.5.1 statement.

**Problem.** The Frobenius-reciprocity adjunction *is* canonical (it's an
adjunction, by definition). The statement "Frobenius reciprocity is
multiplicity-counting, not canonical-map-producing" mis-targets. The
correct content (which the proof actually argues) is that Frobenius
reciprocity *does not pin down* a cross-substrate scar map between
distinct \(F_{21}\)-subreps of a common \(G_2\)-rep.

**Fix.** Rename: "Proposition 4.5.1 (Frobenius reciprocity does not select
a cross-substrate scar map)." Reword statement accordingly.

### S4-6. (C2) ⇏ (C1) sketch needs a concrete witness  *(MINOR)*

**Where.** Theorem 4.4.1 proof, converse paragraph.

**Problem.** A hostile referee will demand a *witness*: two distinct
\(G_2\)-modules whose restrictions to \(F_{21}\) are isomorphic. The proof
does not supply one.

**Fix.** Footnote: a hypothetical biological substrate that carries the
\(F_{21}\)-rep \(L_2 \oplus 2 U_6\) but no extending \(G_2\)-action *is the
canonical case of interest*; or give a representation-theoretic example
within \(G_2\)'s representation ring (e.g., \(V^{14}\) and \(V^{14}\) under
twist by an outer automorphism, when applicable).

### S4-7. \(\mu_k\) on \(I\) vs. on \(S_k\) inconsistency  *(MINOR)*

**Where.** §3.7 / §4.1 vs. §4.4.

**Problem.** \(\mu_k\) is defined as the isotypic profile of the *vector*
scar span \(S_k\subset W\). §4.4 introduces \(I_{\rm syn}, I_{\rm bio}\) as
*operator* spans (real linear span of scar-encoding images in
\(\mathrm{End}(V)\)) and references them in (C2)–(C3). The \(W \leftrightarrow
\mathrm{End}(V)\) translation is unspecified; the \(F_{21}\)-actions differ
(linear vs. adjoint).

**Fix.** Reserve \(\mu_k\) for the vector-level invariant on \(S_k\);
restate (C2)–(C3) as conditions on \(F_{21}\)-isotypic profiles of vector
scar spans, not operator spans. Or add an explicit translation lemma.

### S4-8. Cor 4.4.2 orientation convention is unmotivated  *(NIT)*

**Where.** Corollary 4.4.2 proof.

**Problem.** For absolutely irreducible real \(U_\lambda\),
\(\mathrm{End}_{G_2}(U_\lambda) = \mathbb{R}\); the family of equivariant
maps is \(\mathbb{R}^*\), and unit norm leaves a \(\pm 1\) ambiguity. To pin
down the sign, the proof appeals to "orientation," but no canonical
orientation exists on an abstract irrep.

**Fix.** Acknowledge the residual \(\mathbb{Z}_2\)-sign ambiguity and either
(a) state uniqueness *up to sign*; or (b) supply an extrinsic orientation
rule (e.g., the dynamical-flow direction on each substrate fixes the sign).

## Hostile-reviewer pushback simulation

**§3 — sharpest objection.** "Theorem 3.4.1's hypotheses (R3)–(R4)
effectively assume the conclusion: 'when the dynamics is well-behaved, it
is well-behaved.' (R3) is conditional 'along the trajectory being
considered' but you have established no concrete \((\eta, \kappa, \alpha,
NP_{\rm crit}, C_{\rm crit})\) and \((R_A, R_B, b_A, b_B)\) for which any
trajectory of the augmented flow satisfies (R3)–(R4). The empirical audit
addresses only the \(\kappa = 0\) case where nothing dynamic happens; the
\(\kappa > 0\) regime where Theorem 3.6.1 lives has zero numerical evidence
of well-posedness on a single trajectory."

*Holds?* **Largely yes**, modulo S3-4's fix. Defense: (a) restate (R3) as
parameter-space transversality with Sard-style genericity; (b) verify on at
least one explicit \(\kappa > 0\) trajectory; (c) acknowledge Theorem 3.4.1
as conditional. As written, the theorem's content is under-specified.

**§4 — sharpest objection.** "Your commensurability hierarchy treats
\(F_{21}\)-multiplicity profile-match as necessary for canonical
commensurability, but \(F_{21}\subset G_2\) is *one of many* finite
subgroups. The selection is justified only by citation of Paper 4 — not
reconstructed in §4. Worse: the L1 experimental test specifically tests
\(F_{21}\)-isotypic content, so a substrate carrying a different finite-
subgroup-equivariant structure (e.g., \(\mathbb{Z}_7, D_7\)) would fail L1
for substrate-mismatch reasons orthogonal to the gradual-tear thesis. Your
falsifiability test falsifies the wrong thing."

*Holds?* **Partly.** The choice of \(F_{21}\) is consistent across the
Paper series, but §4 does not recapitulate the justification, and a referee
unfamiliar with Paper 4 will reasonably push back. Fix: one paragraph in
§4.1 stating the inheritance from Paper 4 and acknowledging that
characterizing the smallest finite subgroup for which L1 is *informative*
is open. Defuses the objection without requiring new mathematics.

## Overall verdict

**MAJOR_REVISIONS.**

§3 and §4 together are a strong technical contribution: Schur-circle
inheritance, the four-stratum event geometry, the audit-grounded
\(\kappa = 0\) recovery, the character decomposition, and the commutant-gap
quantification are all real, correct, and well-presented. The framework is
coherent and the scope-fence is honest. However, three structural issues
are too large to wave through a council pass: (1) the boundary-KKT sign
error in Theorem 3.6.1 (S3-1) is *load-bearing* — the headline escape
theorem fails as written for >50% of audited seeds; (2) Theorem 3.4.1's
regularity hypotheses (R3)–(R4) effectively assume the conclusion (S3-4),
so the section's main well-posedness result has no actual content until
restated as a *conditional* theorem with verified hypotheses; (3) Theorem
4.4.1's (C2)⇒(C3) step has a logical gap (S4-1) where (C2) is too weak in
its current form to imply (C3). Each is fixable without reworking the
framework, but each *must* be fixed before the manuscript can stand
top-journal referee scrutiny. Minor issues (commutant-component
bookkeeping, character-prose glitch, notation drift, Cor 4.4.2 orientation
ambiguity) are easily handled in revision. With those corrections plus a
single \(\kappa > 0\) numerical trajectory exhibiting (R3)–(R4), the
section package becomes a clean accept.
