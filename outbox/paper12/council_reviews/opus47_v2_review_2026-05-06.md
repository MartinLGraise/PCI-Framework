Definition 3.2.1 now distinguishes *interior* tear $\xi^{\mathrm{int}}_\star$
from the projected boundary version: "On boundary points $\theta \in
\{0, \pi/2\}$, the tear direction is projected into the inward-pointing
tangent cone $T_{[0,\pi/2]}(\theta)$: $\xi_\star(\theta) :=
\Pi_{T_{[0,\pi/2]}(\theta)}(\xi^{\mathrm{int}}_\star(\theta)).$" Definition
3.3.2 wraps the *entire* RHS $[-\eta\mathcal{F}'_\sigma + \kappa
\mathrm{NP}_\sigma \xi_\star]$ in $\Pi_{T_{[0,\pi/2]}(\theta)}$, so both
the drift and the tear are tangent-cone projected at the boundary —
consistent across Defs 3.2.1, 3.3.2, and Thm 3.6.1.

Crucially, Thm 3.6.1 is now honest: case (iii) explicitly states the
boundary state is a *fixed point* of the augmented flow under the linear
$\xi_\star$, with escape requiring "(a) higher-order coherence
information, or (b) supplementary nonlinear mechanisms beyond the
Schur-derived $\xi_\star$." This is the qualified framing my v1 review
demanded.

The new audit (`paper12_q5_boundary_interior_sup.csv`) confirms: 24/28
INTERIOR_LOSES, 4/28 INTERIOR_BEATS_BOUNDARY. The v2 prose reports this
correctly ("$24/28$ boundary-KKT seeds have $\sup_{\mathrm{interior}} c <

The implication paragraph correctly draws the load-bearing conclusion:
"Theorem 3.6.1(iii)'s 'escape' is therefore not generically a

One small note: the four INTERIOR_BEATS_BOUNDARY seeds include three

### R2 — Theorem 3.4.1 (R3) restated as parameter-space transversality: **CONFIRMED**

The fix is exactly what I asked for. (R3) v2 now reads: "the parameter
septuple $\mathbf{p} := (R_A, R_B, b_A, b_B, \alpha, NP_{\mathrm{crit}},
C_{\mathrm{crit}})$ lies in the open set $\mathcal{P}^* \subset O(N)
\times O(N) \times \mathbb{R}^{2N} \times \mathbb{R}^3_{>0}$ on which all
guard functions $\Delta = c_A - c_B$, $g_\sigma$, $a_\sigma -
NP_{\mathrm{crit}}$, and $q_\sigma := c_\sigma'$ are transverse to zero."
The theorem is renamed "Conditional Regularity," matching the v1 review's
recommendation. The genericity claim — "$\mathcal{P}^*$ is open and dense"
— is now stated explicitly and a Sard-style proof sketch is given:
"each guard function is real-analytic in both $\theta$ and $\mathbf{p}$,
so its critical values are nowhere-dense in the codomain by the
analytic-Sard theorem."

The proof sketch is the right shape but is genuinely a *sketch* — it
asserts the bad parameter set is a "countable union of codimension-$\ge 1$
algebraic strata, hence nowhere dense" without actually computing those
codimensions for each guard. This is acceptable at the level of a
theorem-with-proof-sketch in a structural paper, but a formal version
would need: (i) explicit verification that each guard's vanishing locus
has positive codimension generically (not just analytically — analyticity
plus non-triviality is needed); (ii) that the Filippov-(3.4.A)
$D\Delta$-genericity is preserved under restriction to $\mathcal{P}^*$.
I am willing to call this **CONFIRMED** rather than partial because the
draft is honest about the sketch status (Remark 3.4.2: "Verification of
(R1)–(R4) at a specific parameter point is a finite-dimensional algebraic
check on the guards"), and the conclusion shape matches the v1 demand.

### R3 — Theorem 4.4.1 (C2) reformulated as map existence: **CONFIRMED**

(C2) v2: "There exists an $F_{21}$-equivariant isomorphism $\phi_F:
I_{\mathrm{syn}} \to I_{\mathrm{bio}}$ aligning $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}$

(C2)$\Rightarrow$(C3) gap cleanly: the proof now reads "$\phi_F$ aligns

maps $S_k^{\mathrm{syn}}$ isomorphically onto $S_k^{\mathrm{bio}}$." This
is logically clean.

(C2)$\not\Rightarrow$(C1) witness: "let $V_{\mathrm{syn}} = V^{14}$ ...
$V_{\mathrm{bio}}$ be the $F_{21}$-module $L_2 \oplus 2 U_6$ realized on
a 14-dim biological substrate (e.g., a hypothetical microtubule mode
collection without an extending $G_2$-action)." This is concrete
*enough*: it is a well-defined hypothetical with the right
representation-theoretic structure, and the $19$-dim $U(2) \times U(4)$
parameter family is correctly identified as the source of non-canonicality.
A pickier referee would want the witness to be a constructive
representation (rather than "a hypothetical microtubule mode
collection"), but as a structural converse-failure this suffices.

## Residual issues

**RES-1 (MINOR).** Cor 4.3.2's identification $O_{F_{21}}(W) \cong U(2)
\times U(4)$ via "Restricting $M_2(\mathbb{C})^* \cap U \to U(2)$" is
proved only at the level of dimensions; the actual identification of
the *unitary subgroup* of $M_n(\mathbb{C})$ with $U(n)$ inside the
commutant ring requires an explicit choice of Hermitian form on each
isotypic block. The proof says "the unitary condition uses the natural
Hermitian metric" — fine, but a referee may want one extra sentence
specifying that the metric is the one induced by the $G_2$-invariant
inner product on $W$ restricted to each $F_{21}$-isotypic block. Quick
fix.

**RES-2 (MINOR).** The "operator-vs-vector translation" in §4.4 is
stated as a *convention* ("we assume without loss of generality that
the substrate scar maps factor as $\mathcal{S}_\bullet(e) = v_e \otimes
v_e^* / \|v_e\|^2$"). For multiplicity-one rank-one projectors this is
fine, but the "without loss of generality" is a soft hedge. If real
substrate scar maps are *not* rank-one (e.g., higher-rank corrections
from multi-mode firings), this convention silently restricts the L1
test. Worth a one-sentence acknowledgment in §4.6 that the L1 test
inherits the rank-one convention.

**RES-3 (MINOR).** Theorem 3.4.1 proof sketch asserts that the
sliding field $f_{\mathrm{sl}} = \lambda f_A + (1-\lambda) f_B$ is
real-analytic because "$\lambda$ is determined algebraically from
analytic data." Specifically, $\lambda = D\Delta \cdot f_B / [D\Delta
\cdot (f_B - f_A)]$, which is analytic *provided* the denominator is
nonzero — and that's exactly the $D\Delta$-genericity already in (R3).
So the statement is correct, but the proof would benefit from naming
this denominator condition explicitly to close the circle.

**RES-4 (NIT).** Definition 3.5.1's "Corner-Clarke" item says corner
points are placed in the exceptional stratum, and Theorem 3.5.3 also
puts corners in the exceptional stratum. So corners are in the
exceptional stratum *and* listed as a kind of stratified critical
point — this is mildly self-cross-cutting. Either drop the corner
bullet from Def 3.5.1 (since it's exceptional) or add one sentence
clarifying that this fifth class is the exceptional stratum.

No new MAJOR issues. No old MAJOR issues unresolved.

*Holds?* **Yes, partially.** The objection is real, but the paper
already absorbs it: Remark 3.6.2 explicitly says "Paper 11's analysis
must therefore identify the specific non-Schur nonlinear corrections

trajectory enters a region of strictly higher $c$." The framing is
honest. What a hostile referee would still want is one sentence
quantifying the residual scope: "of the $50$ audited seeds, the
augmented Schur-derived dynamics produces non-trivial trajectories

seeds are fixed points until supplementary mechanisms are introduced."
