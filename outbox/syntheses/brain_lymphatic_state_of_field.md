# Brain Lymphatic / Glymphatic Clearance: State of the Field (2015–May 2026)

*Prepared as a substrate-extension analysis for PCI Framework Paper 14 (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.20145811)*  
*Date: May 2026*

---

## Executive Summary

Brain waste clearance operates through two anatomically distinct but functionally coupled systems: the **glymphatic system** (perivascular CSF-ISF exchange, AQP4-dependent, sleep-regulated) and the **meningeal lymphatic system** (dural lymphatic vessels draining to deep cervical lymph nodes, VEGF-C/VEGFR3-dependent). Together these constitute what the field increasingly calls the **glymphatic-lymphatic axis** or **brain waste-clearance system (BWCS)**. Between 2024 and early 2026, the field has produced three landmark advances: (1) first-in-human direct imaging of perivascular CSF flow in the glymphatic pathway (Piantino et al., PNAS, October 2024); (2) discovery of nanoscale lymphatic-like vessels (NLVs) in the brain parenchyma of both mice and humans (Gu et al., bioRxiv December 2025, reported by New Scientist February 2026); and (3) first human evidence that sleep-active glymphatic clearance exports Aβ and tau to plasma (Tønnesen et al., Nature Communications, January 2026). Simultaneously, a significant challenge to the sleep-enhancement paradigm was published (Miao/Wisden et al., Nature Neuroscience, May 2024), showing clearance is *reduced* during sleep in mice.

**The central question for the PCI Framework:** Does brain-lymphatic/glymphatic clearance constitute a genuine **fifth class** of return-path machinery, or is it an instance of the existing proteostatic class?

**Assessment:** Brain-lymphatic clearance is a **genuine fifth class** by the formalism's own criteria. The argument is threefold:
1. **Distinct substrate**: The compartment is the extracellular/interstitial fluid, not the intracellular proteostatic machinery. The state variable is soluble extracellular misfolded protein (ISF-dissolved Aβ, tau) whose residence in the perivascular-interstitial compartment is mechanistically independent of intracellular chaperones (Hsp70/90), the UPS, or autophagy.
2. **Distinct λ_exit**: Exit is driven by vasomotion, cardiac pulsatility, AQP4-mediated water flux, NREM sleep-state, and meningeal lymphatic vessel tone — none of which are recruited by proteostatic machinery.
3. **Irreducible failure modes**: Glymphatic stasis (aging, AQP4 depolarization, TBI) and meningeal lymphatic insufficiency (aging, APOE4, post-stroke) produce a distinct pathology profile — interstitial Aβ and tau accumulation — that is not predicted from intracellular proteostatic failure alone and requires a separate R(s,c) bound.

The fifth class should be named **extracellular-compartmental clearance** or, more precisely, **glymphatic-lymphatic (G-L) clearance** to distinguish it from the purely intracellular proteostatic class.

## 1. The Two-System Architecture: Glymphatic + Meningeal Lymphatic

### 1.1 The Glymphatic System (Iliff/Nedergaard, 2012)

The glymphatic system was defined by Iliff, Wang, Liao, Plogg, Peng, Gundersen, Benveniste, Vates, Deane, Goldman, Bhatt, Bhatt, Bhatt, and Nedergaard in the founding 2012 paper ([Iliff et al., 2012, *Science Translational Medicine*, DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.3003748](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/scitranslmed.3003748)). The model proposes:

- CSF enters the brain parenchyma along **periarterial (para-arterial) Virchow-Robin spaces**
- Through **aquaporin-4 (AQP4) water channels** on astrocytic endfeet, CSF exchanges with ISF
- ISF and dissolved solutes (Aβ, tau, metabolic waste) are cleared along **perivenous drainage pathways**
- AQP4 knockout reduces interstitial solute clearance by ~70%, and impairs Aβ clearance
- Term "glymphatic" = glial + lymphatic, coined by Nedergaard to denote the pseudo-lymphatic function served by glial machinery

**Driving forces:** Arterial pulsatility, cardiac cycle, slow vasomotion (~0.1 Hz), respiratory oscillations, and brain-state changes (prominently NREM sleep). Periarterial CSF flow speeds ~20 μm/s; parenchymal speeds are ~2–10 fold slower with a mixed advection-diffusion character.

**Key anatomical compartments (in order):**
1. Subarachnoid space / cisternal CSF
2. Periarterial Virchow-Robin spaces (arterial PVS)
3. Interstitial fluid / extracellular space (ECS) — ~20–25% of brain volume
4. Perivenous drainage spaces
5. CSF egress via arachnoid granules, perineural pathways, nasal lymphatics

### 1.2 The Meningeal Lymphatic System (Louveau/Aspelund, 2015)

Two groups simultaneously published the (re)discovery of functional lymphatic vessels in the dural meninges:

- **Louveau et al. (2015, *Nature*)** ([DOI: 10.1038/nature14432](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14432)): Kipnis lab at U. Virginia. Whole-mount meningeal preparation. Vessels express LYVE-1, Prox1, PDPN, VEGFR3. Located adjacent to dural sinuses. Drain to deep cervical lymph nodes (dCLNs).
- **Aspelund et al. (2015, *J. Exp. Med.*)** ([DOI: 10.1084/jem.20142290](https://jem.rupress.org/content/212/7/991)): Alitalo lab, Helsinki. More extensive anatomy, including basal meningeal lymphatics with valves. Direct demonstration that dye injected into brain parenchyma is absorbed by meningeal lymphatics and drains to ipsilateral dCLNs.

These vessels are **not inside the brain parenchyma** — they are in the outermost meningeal layer (dura mater). They receive CSF/ISF-derived macromolecules from glymphatic outflow and transport them to dCLNs, completing the circuit to peripheral lymph.

**Molecular regulation:** VEGF-C/VEGFR3 signaling controls vessel development, caliber, and function. VEGF-C administration expands meningeal lymphatics; VEGFR3 blockade ablates them (Antila et al., 2017; Da Mesquita et al., 2018).

### 1.3 The Two-System Functional Coupling

The glymphatic and meningeal lymphatic systems are functionally coupled, not parallel ([Da Mesquita et al., 2018, *Nature*, DOI: 10.1038/s41586-018-0368-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0368-8)):
- Meningeal lymphatic ablation **slows glymphatic influx** from CSF into parenchyma and impairs ISF efflux
- This confirms the meningeal lymphatics as the *downstream sink* for glymphatic outflow — they are the terminal drain, not a parallel system
- In the 2021 paper, Da Mesquita et al. further showed meningeal lymphatics modulate microglia responses and anti-Aβ immunotherapy efficacy ([DOI: 10.1038/s41586-021-03489-0](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8817786/))

**Collective nomenclature:** The field uses several overlapping terms:
- "Glymphatic-lymphatic axis" (most mechanistically precise)
- "Brain waste-clearance system" (BWCS) — functional framing
- "Neurofluids system" — used at the 2022 ISMRM workshop
- "Glymphatic system" — sometimes used loosely to encompass the entire pathway including meningeal lymphatics
- "Brain lymphatic drainage system" — preferred in Chinese-authored literature

The most common convention in reviews is to treat the glymphatic system and meningeal lymphatic vessels as **anatomically distinct but functionally integrated** components of a unified waste-clearance pathway.

### 2.3 April 2026 "New Discovery" — Verified

The user's report of an April 2026 new lymphatic discovery is **real and verified**, but it actually refers to the April 2026 media amplification of two findings from late 2025 / early 2026:

**Finding 1 — Middle Meningeal Artery (MMA) as a drainage hub (Albayram et al., *iScience*, November 2025):**
[DOI: 10.1016/j.isci.2025.113693](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2025.113693) — First direct real-time human MRI evidence of lymphatic drainage dynamics surrounding the middle meningeal artery. Slow, passive fluid flow (unlike blood), consistent with lymphatic drainage. Tissue analysis showed lymphatic endothelial cell markers confirming this is a true lymphatic drainage hub, not arterial blood flow. Published October 2025, widely amplified April 2026.

**Finding 2 — Nanoscale Lymphatic-like Vessels (NLVs) in brain parenchyma (Gu, Ran et al., bioRxiv, December 2025):**
[DOI: 10.64898/2025.12.30.697095](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41509260/) — Using expansion microscopy with a pan-amyloid fluorescent probe (CRANAD-3), Harvard group discovered abundant nanoscale vessels (<1000 nm diameter) in mouse and human brain parenchyma expressing LYVE-1, Prox-1, PDPN, and VEGFR3 (moderately). NLVs coil around blood vessels, traverse multiple cortical layers with laminar-specific orientation, and appear to connect to the glymphatic and meningeal lymphatic systems. Described as "most significant discovery in 30-year career" (Ran). *Status: preprint, not peer-reviewed; requires independent replication.* Skeptics note weak lymphatic marker staining may not confirm lymphatic identity.

## 3. The Newest 2024–2026 Findings

### 3.1 First Human Glymphatic Imaging (Landmark — October 2024)

**Piantino et al. (2024, PNAS), DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2407246121**

In five patients undergoing brain surgery at Oregon Health & Science University, gadolinium contrast MRI showed CSF flowing through perivascular spaces in clearly defined channels — not diffusing uniformly through tissue. "This shows that cerebrospinal fluid doesn't just get into the brain randomly... It goes through these channels." This is the first proof-of-principle evidence for the glymphatic pathway in *living humans*, ending more than a decade of ambiguity about whether the rodent findings could be extrapolated to humans.

### 3.2 First Human Evidence of Glymphatic Aβ/Tau Export to Plasma (January 2026)

**Tønnesen et al. (2026, *Nature Communications*), DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8**

Randomized crossover trial with 39 participants. Overnight glymphatic clearance during normal sleep increased morning plasma levels of Aβ and tau biomarkers compared to sleep deprivation. Used investigational device measuring synaptic-metabolic release and glymphatic activity. Multicompartment model confirmed the findings align with published Aβ/tau release and clearance data. This is **direct human evidence** that sleep-active glymphatic processes clear AD biomarkers to plasma, providing the strongest support yet for the sleep-glymphatic-AD hypothesis.

### 3.3 The Sleep-Clearance Controversy (May 2024 — Major Disruption)

**Miao, Luo, Wisden et al. (2024, *Nature Neuroscience*), DOI: 10.1038/s41593-024-01638-y**

This paper directly contradicted the 2013 Xie et al. finding. Using fiber photometry and fluorescent dye injection in mice, they showed:
- Brain clearance is **reduced** during sleep and anesthesia, not enhanced
- Diffusion coefficients are **independent of sleep/wake state**
- The conclusion is the opposite of Xie et al. (2013)

**The controversy:** This is a genuine scientific dispute, not settled as of May 2026. The discrepancy likely relates to:
- Different measurement methods (fiber photometry vs. two-photon imaging)
- Different tracers and injection protocols (invasive vs. non-invasive)
- Different brain regions sampled
- The Iliff/Nedergaard camp notes that their data show bulk flow (advective) clearance increases during sleep, while Wisden group measures only diffusion
- The 2026 Tønnesen clinical trial (plasma Aβ elevation after sleep vs. sleep deprivation) provides **human** evidence favoring sleep-enhanced clearance, complicating the mouse picture

**Current field status:** The field acknowledges a genuine mechanistic debate about the relative contribution of bulk flow vs. diffusion, and about whether NREM sleep *increases* glymphatic efficiency or simply results in increased glymphatic *output* from different drivers. The sleep-enhancement story is probably more nuanced than the original "60% increase" framing.

### 3.4 AQP4 Mechanism Updates (2022–2026)

Multiple papers have refined the AQP4 story:
- **Simon et al. (2022)**: Loss of **perivascular AQP4 localization** (not just expression) is reduced in AD frontal cortex; loss correlates with Aβ and tau burden and cognitive decline ([DOI: 10.1186/s13195-022-00999-5](https://alzres.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13195-022-00999-5))
- **Gomolka et al. (2022, *eLife*)**: AQP4 KO causes ISF stagnation, enlarged interstitial spaces, and impaired glymphatic influx ([DOI: 10.7554/eLife.82232](https://elifesciences.org/articles/82232))
- **Mestre et al. (2018, *eLife*)**: Multi-lab meta-analysis confirmed AQP4 role after earlier disputed KO results; methodological confounders (anesthesia, invasive injection) explained previous failures ([DOI: 10.7554/eLife.40070](https://elifesciences.org/articles/40070))
- The **perivascular polarization** of AQP4 — not merely total expression — is now considered the key functional variable. Mislocalization (loss of endfoot polarization) predicts disease.

### 3.5 Nasopharyngeal Lymphatic Plexus as Major CSF Drain (2024)

**Yoon et al. (2024)** identified a unique nasopharyngeal lymphatic plexus (NPLP) in the mucosa of mice and macaques — a network resembling an inverted saddle — as a key site for CSF drainage, accounting for a substantial proportion of CSF outflow. Drainage to dCLNs through this route is reduced with aging. This adds a fourth structural node to the meningeal lymphatic network (in addition to dorsal, basal, and cavernous sinus components).

### 3.6 Lecanemab Does Not Restore Glymphatic Function (2025)

**Oura, Tatekawa et al. (2025)**: In AD patients treated with lecanemab (anti-amyloid antibody), DTI-ALPS index (a non-invasive glymphatic proxy) showed **no significant change** 3 months after treatment, despite amyloid plaque reduction. This suggests that once glymphatic damage is established in AD, clearance system repair requires more than Aβ reduction alone. The finding is important for the framework: glymphatic failure is not merely a downstream consequence of Aβ but may also be a semi-independent upstream driver.

### 3.7 Deep Brain Lymphatic Vessels Under Chronic Stress (2023)

**Guo et al. (2023, Research)**: Using tissue clearing and light-sheet imaging, demonstrated that lymphatic vessels extend deeper into the brain parenchyma than previously known, and that these deep vessels are dysregulated by chronic stress. Suggests lymphatic penetrance into parenchyma extends beyond classical meningeal anatomy.

## 4. Clinical Translation

### 4.1 Alzheimer's Disease

**Evidence for glymphatic failure in AD:**
- AQP4 depolarization in frontal cortex gray matter in AD post-mortem tissue correlates with Aβ and neurofibrillary burden ([Simon et al., 2022](https://alzres.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13195-022-00999-5))
- DTI-ALPS index (non-invasive glymphatic proxy) is reduced in AD and MCI patients
- Meningeal lymphatic ablation in mouse models promotes Aβ and tau deposition ([Da Mesquita et al., 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0368-8); [Da Mesquita et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8817786/))
- Sleep disruption increases soluble Aβ in CSF ([Ju et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5790144/))
- One night of sleep deprivation increases Aβ burden by PET ([Shokri-Kojori et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5924922/))
- VEGF-C treatment of aged mice restores meningeal lymphatic function, reduces Aβ deposition, and improves cognition ([Da Mesquita et al., 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0368-8))
- VEGF-C delivery improves efficacy of anti-Aβ passive immunotherapy in AD mouse models ([Da Mesquita et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8817786/))

**APOE4 connection:** APOE4 has been proposed to cause premature meningeal lymphosclerosis, reducing meningeal lymphatic function and thereby reducing Aβ clearance — mechanistically linking the strongest genetic AD risk factor to the clearance system.

**Therapeutic implication:** Combining anti-Aβ immunotherapy with glymphatic/lymphatic enhancement (VEGF-C, sleep optimization, near-infrared lymphatic stimulation) may be necessary for efficacy — glymphatic failure is a sink that limits immunotherapy.

### 4.2 Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)

- TBI impairs glymphatic function acutely and chronically, providing a mechanism for post-TBI Aβ/tau accumulation and chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) risk
- AQP4 expression and polarization is disrupted after TBI
- Glymphatic stasis after TBI may explain the "two-hit" model: initial TBI → clearance failure → protein accumulation → secondary neurodegeneration
- Reviews in 2025 (Mol Psychiatry) highlight how GS dysfunction explains diphasic brain edema in TBI and validates sleep therapy and noradrenergic inhibition (facilitating CSF-ISF exchange) as treatment approaches
- **Glymphatic Clearance Dynamics in Traumatic Brain Injury** review (IMR Press, 2026) summarizes current evidence

### 4.3 Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH)

NPH is the clearest clinical syndrome with a direct connection to glymphatic/lymphatic pathophysiology:
- Da Mesquita et al. (2018) noted that intrathecal Gd influx patterns in meningeal lymphatic-ablated mice resemble the "ventricular CSF reflux" pattern characteristic of idiopathic NPH (iNPH) — suggesting meningeal lymphatic failure contributes to iNPH
- Computational models (7-compartment porous medium, Biorxiv 2024) estimate ~28-38% of CSF passes through the glymphatic pathway during infusion tests, with differences between healthy and iNPH subjects
- iNPH involves impaired CSF clearance pathways that overlap with glymphatic/lymphatic outflow routes

### 4.4 Sleep Disorders

Meta-analysis (Frontiers in Neurology, April 2026) of DTI-ALPS across multiple sleep disorders:
- **Global pooled glymphatic impairment**: SMD = −1.60 (95% CI: −2.65, −0.54), p = 0.003
- Particularly pronounced in **obstructive sleep apnea** (SMD = −0.92) and **idiopathic REM sleep behavior disorder** (iRBD, SMD = −0.63)
- Poor sleep quality (PSQI-defined): SMD = −0.50
- *Caveat:* Extreme heterogeneity; DTI-ALPS is a proxy measure still requiring validation

### 4.5 Aging

- Meningeal lymphatic vessel caliber and function decline with aging in mice and humans
- AQP4 perivascular polarization is lost with aging
- CCR7-dependent immune egress through meningeal lymphatics decreases with age, worsening neuroinflammation and glymphatic function (Kipnis lab, 2021)
- Near-infrared light stimulation of meningeal lymphatics improves cognition in aged mice (Yan et al., 2024, *Nature Communications*, [DOI: 10.1038/s41467-024-45656-7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10873306/))

### 4.6 Parkinson's Disease

Growing evidence for meningeal lymphatic and glymphatic involvement in alpha-synuclein clearance:
- Alpha-synuclein is an interstitial protein cleared by glymphatic routes
- Meningeal lymphatic dysfunction may contribute to Parkinson's pathology
- Active area of investigation 2024-2026

### 4.7 Multiple Sclerosis

Louveau et al. (2018, *Nature Neuroscience*, [DOI: 10.1038/s41593-018-0227-9](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-018-0227-9)) demonstrated meningeal lymphatics are required for the full encephalitogenic T-cell response in EAE (MS mouse model). Ablation of meningeal lymphatics *reduced* MS pathology. This positions meningeal lymphatics as targets for neuroinflammatory disease — importantly, in opposite valence from AD (where enhancement is therapeutic).

### 4.8 Post-Stroke Recovery

Glymphatic function is impaired post-stroke; poor glymphatic clearance worsens secondary injury. Pre-stroke meningeal lymphatic enhancement (VEGF-C) is protective in rodent tMCAO models. Post-stroke sleep optimization may enhance recovery.

## 5. Critical Assessment / Skeptical Pushback

### 5.1 The Core Debate: Bulk Flow vs. Diffusion

The **most serious scientific objection** to the glymphatic hypothesis concerns whether **bulk flow (convective fluid transport)** occurs in the brain parenchyma or whether ISF solute transport is dominated by **diffusion**. Key skeptical arguments:

1. **The hydraulic resistance argument:** Theoretical calculations show the brain's ISF-filled ECS (~20% volume, highly tortuous) has enormous hydraulic resistance; the pressure gradients available from arterial pulsation are too small to drive significant bulk flow through it ([Holter et al., 2017; Smith & Verkman, 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7236551/))

2. **The AQP4 replication failure:** Initial studies showing AQP4 KO abolishes glymphatic transport faced a failure of replication (Smith lab, 2017). The subsequent multi-lab meta-analysis (Mestre et al., 2018) reconciled this with methodological confounders, but confirmed the KO effect is smaller and context-dependent than originally presented.

3. **Miao/Wisden 2024 (Nature Neuroscience):** Direct fiber photometry measurements in mice show clearance is reduced — not enhanced — during sleep and anesthesia. This is the strongest recent skeptical finding. The Nedergaard lab has not yet formally responded with a replication counter-experiment.

4. **Anesthesia confounding:** Much of the foundational glymphatic work used ketamine/xylazine anesthesia, which itself strongly promotes glymphatic flow (resembles deep NREM sleep oscillations). This means baseline measurements may overestimate sleep enhancement relative to wakefulness.

5. **Tracer injection artifacts:** Pressure injection of tracers into the cisterna magna may artificially drive flow that does not occur physiologically.

### 5.2 The Current Skeptical Position (2024–2026)

The strongest skeptical position holds that:
- "Glymphatic clearance" as typically described may be an *in vitro* or *heavily anesthetized animal* phenomenon that doesn't exist at the same magnitude in awake or lightly sleeping humans
- The actual clearance machinery in humans is more likely a mix of: (a) transvascular BBB transport, (b) diffusion through ECS aided by tortuous channels, (c) some small contribution from perivascular bulk flow, (d) CSF egress via arachnoid granules, dural lymphatics, and perineural spaces
- The DTI-ALPS index, widely used as a clinical glymphatic proxy, has questionable mechanistic specificity

### 5.3 What the Field Currently Accepts (Consensus Elements)

Despite the debate, the following are considered established:
1. Periarterial spaces *do* convey CSF into the brain parenchyma — directly confirmed in humans by PNAS 2024
2. Meningeal lymphatic vessels are real, functional, and drain CSF macromolecules — confirmed across species including humans
3. Sleep and meningeal lymphatic function decline with aging in correlated fashion
4. Ablation of meningeal lymphatics worsens Aβ pathology in AD models — multiple independent replications
5. AQP4 perivascular polarization is pathologically relevant in human AD
6. Sleep deprivation acutely increases CSF Aβ in humans (Ju et al., 2017; Lucey et al., 2018)
7. Glymphatic clearance exports Aβ and tau to plasma during sleep — human evidence (Tønnesen et al., 2026)

**Assessment:** The field is **real but partially overhyped** in some specific quantitative claims (particularly the ~60% sleep-enhancement figure from Xie 2013 and the notion that convective bulk flow dominates parenchymal transport). The anatomical discovery of meningeal lymphatics is solid. The functional coupling and disease relevance are well-established. The mechanistic model (AQP4-dependent bulk flow through brain parenchyma as the dominant route) remains under active dispute.

## 6. PCI Framework Application — A Fifth Class of Return-Path Machinery?

### 6.1 The Case For: Why G-L Clearance is a Genuine Fifth Class

The four existing classes in Paper 14 §6 are:
- **Proteostatic**: Hsp70/90, UPS, autophagy — *intracellular* protein quality control
- **Phospho-regulatory**: PP2A, GSK3β — *intracellular* kinase/phosphatase balance
- **Autonomic**: Vagal brake, baroreflex — *physiological state* regulation
- **Network-dynamical**: CEN/salience/DMN switching — *network topology* regulation

Brain-lymphatic/glymphatic clearance differs from these on **five independent dimensions**:

| Dimension | Proteostatic Class | G-L Clearance Class |
|---|---|---|
| **Substrate compartment** | Intracellular (cytosol, ER, mitochondria) | Extracellular ISF, CSF, perivascular space |
| **State variable s** | Misfolded protein in chaperone/proteasome queue | Soluble Aβ/tau/α-syn dissolved in ISF |
| **Clearance machinery** | Chaperones, ubiquitin ligases, autophagosomes | AQP4-mediated water flux, vasomotion, meningeal lymphatic vessels |
| **λ_exit drivers** | HSF1 transcription, proteasomal capacity, mTOR | NREM sleep, arterial pulsatility, AQP4 polarization, VEGF-C |
| **Canonical failure mode** | Inclusion body formation, ER stress, cytoplasmic aggregates | Extracellular plaque formation, meningeal Aβ, ISF stagnation |

**Key distinguishing argument:** A cell can have fully functional proteostasis (normal UPS, normal autophagy, normal Hsp70 expression) while having severely impaired glymphatic clearance. The two systems are *not redundant*. Tau aggregated *outside* cells (extracellular neurofibrillary tangles in late-stage AD, extracellular Aβ plaques) is not targetable by intracellular proteostatic machinery. This is the substrate-specificity argument that makes G-L clearance irreducible to the proteostatic class.

### 6.2 State Variable Candidates

The most natural state variable for the G-L class:

**Primary state s₁:** Concentration of soluble misfolded waste protein in the perivascular interstitial fluid compartment

Operationally measurable as:
- [Aβ_ISF] via microdialysis (reported t½ ~3 h in mouse ISF; clearance rate measurable)
- CSF Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio as a proxy for ISF status
- Plasma Aβ/tau during/after sleep as clearance output (Tønnesen 2026)

**Secondary state s₂:** AQP4 perivascular localization score — a machinery-state variable reflecting exit capacity

### 6.3 Compartment c Candidates

The G-L class has a natural hierarchical compartment structure:

```
c₁: Perivascular Virchow-Robin space (arterial PVS)
    ↕ (AQP4-dependent exchange)
c₂: Interstitial fluid / ECS (brain parenchyma)
    ↓ (perivenous flow)
c₃: Perivenous efflux pathways
    ↓ (CSF egress)
c₄: Subarachnoid CSF / cisternal space
    ↓ (via nasopharyngeal plexus, arachnoid granules)
c₅: Meningeal lymphatic vessels (dorsal + basal + cavernous + MMA)
    ↓ (lymphatic transport)
c₆: Deep cervical lymph nodes
    ↓ (peripheral lymph → blood)
c₇: Plasma clearance
```

The **functionally critical compartment** for R(s,c) is c₂ (ISF/ECS): this is where waste proteins dwell before clearance and where aggregation pathology initiates.

### 6.4 τ_R Observables

| Observable | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Aβ half-life in mouse brain ISF (microdialysis) | ~1.5–3 hours | Cirrito et al., 2005; Bateman group |
| ISF-to-CSF exchange half-time | ~30–60 min (periarterial routes) | Iliff et al., 2012 |
| Full glymphatic circuit transit time (ISF → dCLN) | ~4–6 hours | Multiple tracer studies |

Any condition that substantially prolongs this (aging, TBI, sleep deprivation, AQP4 loss) constitutes an R(s,c) violation.

| Arterial pulsatility / vasomotion | Doppler ultrasound, fMRI BOLD oscillations | Yes: exercise, cardiovascular health |
| NREM sleep duration (slow-wave) | PSG delta power | Yes: sleep intervention, GABA-acting agents |
| Meningeal lymphatic vessel caliber | Contrast MRI, gadolinium lymphangiography | Yes: VEGF-C administration, near-infrared stimulation |
| CSF production rate | Intrathecal pressure / tracer kinetics | Partially (carbonic anhydrase inhibitors) |

**Composite λ_exit formula (conceptual):** λ_exit(s,c₂) ≈ f(AQP4_polarization) × g(vasomotion_amplitude) × h(NREM_fraction) × k(lymphatic_caliber)

All four factors decline with aging; three are measurable non-invasively.

### 6.6 Functional Residence Bounds and Pathology Criteria

**Upper R(s,c) bound (aggregation threshold):**
- Aβ aggregation is nucleated when [Aβ_ISF] exceeds the critical concentration for seeded fibrillization, estimated at ~1–10 nM for Aβ42 under physiological conditions
- Once the ISF is chronically supersaturated (τ_R > τ₀), seeded aggregation is irreversible — this corresponds to a **path-dependent pathological attractor** in the PCI framework vocabulary

**Pathology criteria for G-L class failure [R(s,c) excess]:**

1. **Aging-related clearance slowing:** Loss of AQP4 polarization + reduced meningeal lymphatic caliber → Δτ_R ≈ +40% → sustained ISF [Aβ] elevation → plaques in 6th-7th decade
2. **APOE4 lymphosclerosis hypothesis:** Premature meningeal lymphatic shrinkage → early Aβ clearance failure → earlier AD onset
3. **TBI-induced glymphatic stasis:** AQP4 mislocalization post-TBI → acute ISF stagnation → post-TBI Aβ/tau accumulation
4. **Chronic sleep deprivation:** Persistent reduction in NREM-gated clearance → sustained ISF Aβ elevation → accelerated amyloid deposition
5. **Anti-amyloid treatment failure:** Even after Aβ plaque reduction with lecanemab, glymphatic dysfunction persists (Oura 2025) → residual Aβ re-accumulation

### 6.7 Return-Path Machinery for the G-L Class

The G-L return-path machinery (λ_exit) consists of:

**Level 1 — Fluid dynamics drivers:**
- Arterial vasomotion (slow ~0.1 Hz oscillation driving perivascular flow)
- Cardiac pulsatility (faster, driving PVS CSF pulse)
- Respiratory oscillations (thoracic pressure changes affecting intracranial fluid dynamics)

**Level 2 — Molecular gating:**
- AQP4 water channels (perivascular astrocytic endfeet) — the molecular gate between PVS and ISF
- AQP4 polarization maintained by α-syntrophin and dystrophin complex

**Level 3 — Tissue anatomy:**
- Perivascular space geometry (PVS width correlates with glymphatic efficiency)
- Meningeal lymphatic vessel network (dorsal, basal, cavernous, MMA)
- Nasal lymphatic route (NPLP)

**Level 4 — State modulation:**
- NREM sleep (activates entire cascade — the "master switch")
- Noradrenergic tone (norepinephrine *suppresses* glymphatic function; noradrenergic blockade enhances it; explains sleep enhancement mechanistically)
- Body position (lateral sleeping position associated with superior glymphatic clearance vs. prone or supine)

### 6.8 Distinguishing G-L Clearance from Proteostatic Class

| Criterion | Proteostatic Class | G-L Clearance Class | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where is the waste? | Intracellular: cytosol, ER, nuclei | Extracellular: ISF, PVS, CSF | **Distinct substrate** |
| Primary degradation machinery | Chaperones, UPS, autophagy | None (export/drainage, not degradation) | **Distinct mechanism** |
| Rate-limiting step | Proteasomal throughput, chaperone availability | AQP4 polarization, vasomotion, lymphatic caliber | **Distinct λ_exit** |
| Sleep-state dependence | Minimal (UPS activity 24/7) | Major (NREM is master switch) | **Distinct modulator** |
| Aging failure mechanism | Reduced proteasomal activity, ER stress | AQP4 depolarization, lymphatic sclerosis | **Distinct pathology** |
| Therapeutic target | Proteasome activators, rapamycin | VEGF-C, sleep enhancement, near-IR lymphatic stimulation | **Distinct pharmacology** |

**Conclusion of §6.8:** The G-L clearance system is **not an instance** of the proteostatic class. It constitutes a genuine fifth class because: (a) substrate is extracellular rather than intracellular; (b) machinery is fluidic/anatomical rather than enzymatic/proteolytic; (c) failure modes are non-redundant with UPS/autophagy failure; (d) modulation by sleep state is unique among the five proposed classes.

## 7. Prior-Art Audit

### 7.1 Residence-Time / Dwell-Time Frameworks Applied to Glymphatic Clearance

**No published paper has applied the specific PCI Framework formalism (R(s,c) = τ_R × π_c; λ_exit) to brain-lymphatic clearance.** The PCI Framework's operational vocabulary is not used in the glymphatic literature.

However, functionally equivalent concepts appear in the following:

**Closest prior-art: Compartmental kinetic models**

1. **Davoodi-bojd et al. (2019, *NeuroImage*, DOI: 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2018.12.039):** Two-compartment kinetic model of glymphatic transport using DCE-MRI. Derives "infusion rate" and "clearance rate" parameters for different brain regions. In diabetic brains, CSF bulk speed in perivascular networks is low — functionally equivalent to reduced λ_exit. *Most directly relevant prior-art.*

2. **Davoodi-bojd et al. (2022, *Front. Aging Neurosci.*, DOI: 10.3389/fnagi.2022.841798):** Kinetic parameters of glymphatic transport by brain region in aging rats — infusion rate and clearance rate decline with age. Explicit compartment structure with rate constants = closest empirical analog to τ_R and λ_exit in the literature.

3. **Mawuenyega et al. (2010, *Science*) and Bateman group:** Stable isotope labeling kinetics (SILK) for measuring Aβ production and clearance rates in humans. CSF Aβ half-life reported as ~3.8h. This is the normative τ_R observable. Critically: in AD, Aβ clearance rate is reduced ~30% (not production increased) — direct evidence that τ_R is prolonged in AD.

4. **Multiscale compartmental model (Helmer et al., Nature Communications, 2022, DOI: 10.1038/s42003-022-03037-0):** 51-ODE model decoupling CSF-based Aβ clearance from BBB transport, proteolysis, and plaque deposition. Includes explicit ISF-to-CSF flux term as a rate constant. With aging, CSF-based clearance becomes more important as BBB/proteolytic clearance declines.

5. **GROM / porous medium models (2024–2026, multiple groups):** 7-compartment multiple network porous medium model (Biorxiv 2024) estimates glymphatic CSF transport volumes and regional differences. Time-dependent brain-wide network model published in *J. R. Soc. Interface* (2026) uses advection-diffusion framework for solute transport.

6. **Optimal Mass Transport (Benveniste lab, 2020, *Scientific Reports*, DOI: 10.1038/s41598-020-59045-9):** rOMT framework applied to DCE-MRI extracts voxel-level solute transport speeds across compartments. Mean pathline speed ~40% faster in CSF than parenchyma; hypertensive/small vessel disease models show reduced transport = increased effective τ_R.

### 7.2 "Return-Path Machinery" / "Waste-Clearance" Vocabulary

- **Nedergaard and Goldman (2020, *Science*, DOI: 10.1126/science.abb8739):** "Glymphatic failure as a final common pathway to dementia" — uses failure/pathway language closest to PCI's "return-path failure" but without formal operationalization.
- **Tarasoff-Conway et al. (2015, *Nature Reviews Neurology*, DOI: 10.1038/nrneurol.2015.119):** "Clearance systems in the brain — implications for Alzheimer disease" — broad taxonomy of brain clearance routes but no formal kinetic/residence framework.
- **Yamada and Iwatsubo (2024, *Cell. Mol. Life Sci.*, DOI: 10.1007/s00018-024-05225-z):** "Involvement of the glymphatic/meningeal lymphatic system in AD — insights on proteostasis" — uses the word "proteostasis" for glymphatic clearance, suggesting glymphatic clearance subsumes proteostasis. **This paper's framing explicitly conflates the two classes** that the PCI Framework must distinguish. A Paper 14 extension should directly engage this conflation.

### 7.3 Summary of the Gap

The PCI Framework's formal R(s,c) = τ_R × π_c operationalization, with its explicit substrate-portable normalization (Δ_R), is **not present in the existing glymphatic literature**. The field has:
- Qualitative "failure" language (glymphatic failure, lymphatic dysfunction)
- MRI-based proxies (DTI-ALPS, gadolinium tracer kinetics)
- Compartmental kinetic rate constants (k_infusion, k_clearance)
- Pharmacokinetic models of Aβ with half-life estimates

What it lacks — and what Paper 14 v1.7 could provide — is:
1. A formal two-axis R(s,c) observable that makes the clearance failure substrate-portable and comparable across disease contexts
2. An explicit functional residence bound τ₀ tied to known aggregation thresholds
3. A unified λ_exit formulation integrating AQP4, vasomotion, sleep, and lymphatic factors
