## Executive Summary

The microtubule–quantum consciousness program entered 2026 in its strongest empirical position since Penrose and Hameroff introduced Orch-OR in the early 1990s — but "strongest position" means a cluster of suggestive experiments, not a confirmed theory. Three developments define the current moment:

**1. The Wellesley epothilone B result (Khan et al., eNeuro, 2024).** Rats injected with the blood-brain-barrier-penetrant microtubule stabilizer epothilone B (0.75 mg/kg) took a mean 69 seconds longer to lose the righting reflex under isoflurane anesthesia (n = 8; p = 0.0016; Cohen's d = 1.9 — a large effect). Lead author Sana Khan, PI Michael Wiest (Wellesley College). The paper explicitly invokes Orch-OR; the effect has since been replicated in mice (Wiest lab, Neuropharmacology, April 2026, epoB 8 mg/kg, Cohen's d = 0.8 within-subject). Classical alternative explanations (synaptic disruption via impaired axonal transport) have not been ruled out, but no published paper has demonstrated the mechanistic classical pathway.

**2. Superradiance in microtubule tryptophan networks (Babcock, Kurian et al., J. Phys. Chem. B, 2024).** Collective superradiant states confirmed experimentally at room temperature in microtubule-sized tryptophan lattices. Crucially, "bright" (superradiant) states have lifetimes of hundreds of femtoseconds, while "dark" (subradiant) states extend to tens of seconds — a qualitative departure from single-chromophore estimates. These findings were independently active at in-vitro level; no in-vivo measurement yet.

**3. The May 2026 Frontiers in Psychology critical review (Ma and Wang, Xiamen University).** The most authoritative 2026 assessment of the field concludes: quantum theories are "becoming more experimentally grounded" but none have cleared the scientific obstacles needed to explain subjective experience. Direct quote: "Quantum mechanisms, therefore, look, at the current stage, more like potential realizers of consciousness than like complete theories of consciousness." Quote on Orch-OR specifically: "Current expositions of Orch OR tend to remain at the level of an intuition: if there are quantum processes, novel conscious states may arise, without stating a clear rule of derivation from quantum-state dynamics to the what-it-is-likeness of experience."

For the PCI Framework: No prior literature uses residence-time / coupling-failure / exit-rate vocabulary for microtubule coherence states. The conceptual territory is clean. The Babcock superradiant/subradiant lifetime spread (femtoseconds to tens of seconds) is the natural τ_R analog. The anesthetic "disruption of coherent quantum state" is directly reframeable as λ_exit pathological elevation. This is genuine claim territory.

## 1. State of the Empirical Evidence (May 2026)

### 1.1 What the evidence actually shows

The evidentiary base has four layers, each with a different epistemic weight:

**Layer A — In vitro, room temperature quantum effects in isolated microtubules (highest confidence)**

- **Electronic energy migration (Kalra / Kurian lab, ACS Central Science, 2023):** UV-induced exciton propagation in purified microtubules exceeded classical expectations in range (up to 6.6 nm) and duration. Propagation was inhibited by clinically relevant anesthetics (isoflurane, etomidate) at physiologically relevant concentrations. This is the "Kalra et al. 2023" paper cited repeatedly in the field as showing anesthetic dampening of quantum optical effects.

- **UV superradiance from tryptophan mega-networks (Babcock, Montes-Cabrera, Oberhofer, Chergui, Celardo, Kurian; J. Phys. Chem. B, 2024):** Theoretical prediction of strongly superradiant states due to collective interactions among >10^5 tryptophan UV-excited dipoles in microtubule architectures, confirmed experimentally via fluorescence quantum yield enhancement. The quantum yield enhancement is robust even at thermal equilibrium and under five-fold greater disorder than physiological levels. Critical timing data: superradiant (bright) states, ~hundreds of femtoseconds; subradiant (dark) states, up to tens of seconds. These are at room temperature. The gap — nine orders of magnitude from Hodgkin-Huxley millisecond signaling — makes direct consciousness timing claims highly speculative.

- **Important specificity — singlet vs. triplet energy migration:** A 2024 paper on triplet energy migration in cytoskeletal polymers concluded that **singlet** energy migration dominates over **triplet** migration in these systems. This does not kill the microtubule program, but it does mean the present empirical center of gravity is better described as **UV-optical excitonic transport and radiative coupling** rather than as a broad validation of every microtubule "quantum channel" claim at once. The framework's vocabulary should anchor on the optical-excitonic (singlet) substrate where the evidence is strongest, not on speculative triplet-mediated cascades.

**Layer B — In living neurons, ex vivo (moderate confidence)**

- **MT resonance spanning neurons (Saxena et al., 2020; Singh et al., 2021a,b; Bandyopadhyay lab, NIMS):** Stimulated microtubule resonances in cultured neurons at specific AC frequencies. The resonance state was reported to span across multiple neurons and to control membrane voltage. These findings have not been independently replicated. Wiest (2025) cites them as among the strongest in-vivo evidence, while the Frontiers in Psychology review (Ma and Wang, 2026) notes they come from "simplified laboratory systems rather than living human brains."

- **Honeybee brain oscillations driven by microtubules (Gutierrez et al., Frontiers in Molecular Neuroscience, 2021):** Patch-clamping of honeybee brain MT sheets showed spontaneous electrical oscillations at ~38 Hz and ~93 Hz, consistent with oscillations from mammalian MT preparations. Triton X permeabilization of whole brain revealed the same frequencies; paclitaxel abolished them. This is the cleanest evidence that MT electrical oscillations contribute to LFP in an intact (if non-mammalian) brain preparation.

**Layer C — Pharmacological, indirect (suggestive but mechanistically ambiguous)**

- **Epothilone B anesthesia delay (Khan et al., eNeuro, 2024; replicated Wiest lab mice, Neuropharmacology, 2026):** See Executive Summary. The critical interpretive ambiguity: isoflurane binds to many targets. EpoB stabilizes microtubules but also has off-target effects. The paper identifies no direct measurement of altered quantum coherence in the epoB-treated animals; the argument is purely behavioral (LORR latency). Classical route: epoB could affect consciousness by protecting MT-mediated axonal transport of synaptic vesicles, preserving the classical signaling machinery that isoflurane disrupts. The authors acknowledge this but note no evidence exists for this specific classical pathway.

- **Quantum chemical modeling (Craddock et al., 2015, 2017):** Anesthetic potencies predicted by binding affinity to delocalized electron sites within tubulin. This "essentially reproduces the Meyer-Overton correlation" using the tubulin binding model — a genuinely notable result. The Wiest (2025) review notes this "cannot be said for any other candidate molecular target."

- **Chemotherapy anesthesia resistance in humans (Linganna et al., 2015):** Patients on MT-stabilizing chemotherapy (not specified as epothilone in available data) showed slight resistance to anesthesia. Small study; not designed to test quantum consciousness.

**Layer D — In vivo human brain (weakest, most contested)**

- **Kerskens and Pérez MRI entanglement (Journal of Physics Communications, 2022):** Used zero-quantum coherence NMR protocol to detect "non-classical" signals in conscious human brains correlated with heartbeat-evoked potentials, short-term memory, and conscious state vs. sleep. The signal appeared only in conscious individuals. Critics argued immediately that the signals could reflect conventional physiological artifacts associated with heartbeat and blood flow. The Ma and Wang (2026) review reports: "Independent replications have not yet confirmed the findings, and the debate has become a case study in the difficulty of separating genuine quantum signals from ordinary biological noise." Hameroff has cited this as strong evidence; the broader field treats it as preliminary.

### 1.2 Strongest counter-evidence

1. **The decoherence timeline.** Tegmark (2000) calculated coherence times of ~10^-13 s for microtubule superpositions — thirteen orders of magnitude shorter than the ~100 ms neural timescales Orch-OR requires. Hagan et al. (2002) corrected Tegmark's assumptions (wrong superposition separation distance, assumed thermal equilibrium "equivalent to death") and pushed estimates to 10^-5 to 10^-4 s. Revised QED-cavity models (Mavromatos and colleagues, 2025) reach 10^-6 s. The Frontiers in Human Neuroscience (2025) review by Sergi et al. reports that experimentally observed coherence times in microtubules from Saxena et al. (2020) are in the 10^-6 to 10^-4 s range — consistent with Hagan-corrected estimates, but still three orders of magnitude short of neural decision timescales (~300 ms). The timing gap is reduced, not closed, unless mechanisms requiring only brief quantum events (rather than sustained coherence) are invoked.

2. **Layer-A evidence is in vitro only.** The Babcock superradiance and Kalra energy migration results are in purified microtubule preparations, not in intact neurons, not in vivo. In-vivo conditions (cytoplasmic protein crowding, MAP binding, GTP hydrolysis dynamics, intracellular calcium transients) may substantially alter both the presence and duration of quantum optical effects.

3. **Gassab et al. (Entropy, 2025) verdict on microtubules:** Running quantum information science analysis, the authors found Posner molecules (Fisher model) were the most theoretically coherent quantum consciousness candidate; microtubules "still look a bit speculative" in the words of lead author Gassab (Popular Mechanics, April 2026). The direct quote from Gassab: "Their lattice-like geometry makes them attractive for studying collective physical phenomena, yet the evidence tying them to cognition or consciousness just isn't there yet. Microtubules are good candidates to study quantum effects in biology, but any connection to brain behavior remains hypothetical and must be approached with caution."

4. **No in vivo quantum measurement of microtubules in conscious animals.** Every quantum-relevant measurement has been either in vitro, ex vivo (isolated brain/MT preparations), or indirect (behavioral pharmacology). The field has no direct in-vivo quantum measurement in a conscious animal.

5. **Alternative mechanisms for every finding.** For the anesthesia results, alternatives include classical synaptic transmission disruption via impaired MT-based vesicle transport. For the Kerskens MRI result, heartbeat artifacts and blood-flow effects. For Bandyopadhyay resonances, the findings have not been independently replicated.

### 1.3 Field consensus shift 2023–2026

The 2024–2026 period represents a genuine shift from "quantum consciousness is pseudoscience" to "quantum consciousness is a serious research program that has not yet cleared its evidentiary bar." The April 2026 Frontiers in Human Neuroscience editorial (Wiest and Gupta) notes that the hypothesis "has been considered laughable since it was proposed by Nobel Laureate Roger Penrose and others in the 1990s" but that "in recent years evidence has accumulated from multiple sources to support the physical plausibility of the quantum consciousness hypothesis." The word "plausibility" is precise — the shift is from implausible to possibly physically permissible, not from unproven to confirmed. The Ma and Wang (2026) review explicitly endorses this framing: "the field is slowly maturing from speculative theory into a more stringent scientific enterprise."

### 1.4 Pending / in-progress experiments

- **Extended epoB studies:** Wiest lab explicitly planning larger mammalian studies. The April 2026 mouse replication paper notes "reduced effects on subsequent days" after a single injection, opening dosing/schedule questions.
- **THz spectroscopy of neurons under anesthesia:** Multiple labs attempting to use THz spectrometers on thin tissue slices (not intact brain) to correlate MT quantum optical signals with anesthetic state. No published results as of May 2026.
- **NV-diamond quantum sensors:** A 2026 review in the Journal of Biological Physics documents advances in nitrogen-vacancy center diamond quantum sensors for brain mapping with nanoscale resolution and microsecond temporal resolution. This technology is approaching but has not yet achieved neural in-vivo measurement.
- **Isotope experiments:** Ma and Wang (2026) note that modern quantum consciousness researchers are increasingly proposing "measurable hypotheses involving anesthesia, isotope substitutions, fluorescence signals, and cutting-edge imaging techniques." No isotope-substitution consciousness experiment on microtubules specifically has been published as of May 2026.

## 2. The Terahertz Detection Program — Who, What, Where

### 2.1 What the Popular Mechanics April 2026 article actually says

The April 21, 2026 Popular Mechanics article ("To Find Consciousness, Scientists Are Turning to Terahertz Waves," by Stav Dimitropoulos) frames THz scanning as a prospective tool, not a current capability. Key factual content:

- THz waves can read "subtle vibrational 'fingerprints' of molecules without the damaging effects of x-rays," useful for soft structures like microtubules.
- **Current state:** THz tools have helped with prepared samples (thin tissue slices). Goal is to eventually track MT shifts inside the intact brain without interference.
- **Maryland attribution:** The article refers to "researchers at the University of Maryland" giving rats "compounds that stabilize microtubules" in 2024. This appears to be a misattribution or conflation — the actual study was conducted at Wellesley College (Khan et al., eNeuro, 2024). The article explicitly notes that team "did not use terahertz scanners" — they "altered the microtubules directly."
- **Gassab quote on THz experiments:** "For them to be convincing, though, they need to be reproducible, to exclude trivial effects like heating or scattering, and to show that the signal is genuinely linked to neural function and not just a side effect. At the moment, they raise interesting possibilities, but more evidence is required before they can be seen as proof."
- The article invokes a prospective scenario: "If upcoming studies show that terahertz waves can reliably detect this faint molecular activity inside the working brain, these scanners could become the first noninvasive window into the quantum heartbeat of awareness."

**Assessment:** The Popular Mechanics piece is aspirational framing, not a report of completed THz brain-scanning experiments. There are no published results of in-vivo THz measurement of microtubule quantum states in a conscious animal as of May 2026.

### 2.2 Who is doing THz spectroscopy on biological microtubules

**Active groups as of 2024–2026:**

- **Kurian lab (Howard University / Quantum Biology Laboratory):** Primary group for quantum optical studies of MT tryptophan networks. The Babcock et al. (2024) paper used UV fluorescence spectroscopy (not strictly THz) to demonstrate superradiance. The group models MT-scale tryptophan networks and has published on quantum photoprotection in actin and amyloid fibrils (Frontiers in Physics, 2024). Working in UV-vis and near-UV regimes; proposed THz extensions are theoretical.

- **Bandyopadhyay lab (NIMS, Tsukuba, Japan):** The foundational group for electrical resonance measurements of MTs and neurons. They measure resonance spectra from kHz to THz using dielectric spectroscopy — not THz beam illumination but impedance-based frequency sweeps. Their published claims include a "triplet of triplets" self-similar resonance hierarchy spanning Hz to THz in tubulin, MT, and neuron. The Dodecanogram (DDG) device measures brain signals from Hz to 6 THz (see Section 5).

- **THz tissue spectroscopy groups (primarily oncology):** Multiple groups (reported in Neural Regeneration Research, 2024; iScience, 2025) are using THz spectroscopy for brain tissue characterization — glioma margins, hydration mapping, cancer detection. These groups are not working on quantum consciousness; they are using THz for structural/compositional tissue analysis. Their work is relevant as proof-of-principle that THz can distinguish brain tissue types ex vivo.

- **Craddock / Tuszynski (Alberta/Rome):** Computational group that modeled collective terahertz oscillations in tubulin dimers and their alteration by anesthetics (Craddock et al., 2017). Primarily computational/theoretical; have proposed that anesthetics shift the 613 THz collective dipole oscillation.

### 2.3 Technical barriers to in-vivo THz measurement

1. **Penetration depth:** THz radiation is strongly absorbed by water. In biological tissue, useful penetration depth is ~0.1–1 mm — nowhere near deep enough to reach cortical or subcortical neurons in intact skulls in vivo. All current biological THz experiments require ex vivo thin tissue sections or surface-accessible structures.

2. **Signal-to-noise at physiological temperatures:** Thermal noise at 310K produces blackbody radiation that overlaps with THz frequencies, making detection of low-power biological THz signals extremely challenging without cryo conditions.

3. **Spatial resolution vs. quantum scale:** Even if penetration were achieved, individual microtubule quantum states (nanometer scale) are far below diffraction-limited THz resolution (~100 μm at 3 THz). Bulk measurements would average over millions of microtubules, washing out single-filament quantum events.

4. **Distinguishing quantum from classical vibrations:** MT vibrations at THz frequencies can occur classically (thermal phonons). Distinguishing quantum-coherent THz oscillations from classical thermal oscillations in an intact tissue requires additional observables (coherence length measurements, quantum yield, entanglement witnesses) not achievable with conventional THz spectrometers.

5. **Motion artifacts:** The brain is a metabolically active, mechanically dynamic tissue. Blood flow pulsations, breathing, and cellular movements create THz-range artifacts.

### 2.4 What a positive result would mean

A successful in-vivo THz measurement would require: (a) detection of a THz signal from identified MT structures in intact neurons; (b) correlation of signal modulation with conscious vs. unconscious states; (c) anesthetic suppression of the signal; (d) isotope-substitution controls to rule out classical vibrational modes. If all four conditions were met, it would establish that MT quantum optical states are genuinely correlated with consciousness — though still not proving causation. It would represent the first direct physical measurement of a quantum state in a neural microtubule in vivo, which would be a landmark result regardless of its consciousness implications.

## 3. The 2024 Wellesley/Maryland Anesthesia Result — Deep Dive

### 3.1 The actual paper

**Full citation:** Khan S, Huang Y, Timuçin D, Bailey S, Lee S, Lopes J, Gaunce E, Mosberger J, Zhan M, Abdelrahman B, Zeng X, Wiest MC. "Microtubule-Stabilizer Epothilone B Delays Anesthetic-Induced Unconsciousness in Rats." *eNeuro.* 2024 Aug 27;11(8):ENEURO.0291-24.2024. DOI: 10.1523/ENEURO.0291-24.2024

**Lead/corresponding author:** Sana Khan (first author); Michael C. Wiest (PI/corresponding, mwiest@wellesley.edu)

**Institution:** Neuroscience Department, Wellesley College, Wellesley, Massachusetts 01760

**Note on "University of Maryland":** The Popular Mechanics piece (April 2026) attributes this work to "the University of Maryland." This appears to be an error. All available primary source documentation (PMC, Newswise, LinkedIn, eNeuro) identifies the institution as Wellesley College. One 2025 commentary by Wiest himself (Neurosci Conscious, 2025) also consistently identifies Wellesley College.

### 3.2 Drug

**Epothilone B (epoB)** — a blood-brain-barrier-penetrant microtubule-stabilizing compound. Not paclitaxel (Taxol), which does not cross the blood-brain barrier. This distinction is critical: the BBB-penetrance of epoB is what makes the behavioral result interpretable as a CNS effect. Paclitaxel would not reach neurons in the CNS at the doses used.

**Dose:** 0.75 mg/kg subcutaneous (rats); 8 mg/kg subcutaneous (mice, 2026 replication).

**Anesthetic:** Isoflurane 4%, in oxygen, flow rate 1.5 L/min.

### 3.3 Methodology

- **Design:** Within-subject crossover. 8 male Long-Evans rats. Pre-epoB sessions served as baseline; post-epoB sessions as experimental. Injection given after baseline LORR measurement was recorded (preserving measurement integrity).
- **Primary outcome:** Latency to loss of righting reflex (LORR) — the standard rodent marker for unconsciousness.
- **Blinding:** At least two researchers present for every experiment; injection assignment blinded to LORR measurers.
- **Tolerance controls:** Separate group of 4 rats run through multiple isoflurane sessions without epoB to estimate tolerance effects from repeated isoflurane exposure.

### 3.4 Results

- Mean LORR latency increase: **+69 seconds** (post-epoB vs. pre-epoB)
- Statistical significance: p = 0.0016 (two-tailed permutation t-test, N = 8)
- Effect size: **Cohen's d = 1.9** (large by conventional criteria)
- Confidence interval: [37.4, 109] seconds
- After omitting 8 identified outliers: p = 0.005, Cohen's d = 5.4

The **2026 mouse replication** (Wiest lab, Neuropharmacology, April 2026, epoB 8 mg/kg): 29-second within-subject LORR increase, Cohen's d = 0.8 (smaller effect, still statistically significant within-subject). The smaller effect may reflect the different species, dose, or the daily dosing protocol used (reduced effects on subsequent days noted).

### 3.5 The quantum vs. classical interpretive question

The paper concludes with its significance statement: "Our study establishes that action on intracellular microtubules (MTs) is the mechanism, or one of the mechanisms, by which the inhalational anesthetic gas isoflurane induces unconsciousness in rats."

The quantum interpretation: epoB stabilizes the MT quantum coherent state against isoflurane disruption, delaying the collapse of the quantum substrate of consciousness.

**Classical alternatives the paper addresses:**
1. **Synaptic disruption route:** Isoflurane could disrupt consciousness by impairing synaptic transmission, which depends on axonal transport (MT-dependent). epoB would protect the transport machinery. The authors note this route is "conceivable" but that "no evidence points to MTs as the cause of isoflurane-induced disruption of synaptic transmission" to date.
2. **Direct membrane effects:** Isoflurane potentiates GABA_A receptor activity. Mice with nonfunctional GABA_A receptors are "somewhat" but not fully resistant to isoflurane, suggesting it is one of several targets.
3. **Mitochondrial inhibition:** Isoflurane inhibits mitochondrial complex I (Zimin et al., 2018); this is a potential MT-independent route.
4. **VLPO activation:** Isoflurane activates sleep-promoting hypothalamic ventrolateral preoptic neurons; again MT-independent.

The paper does not rule out classical mechanisms. The lead author (PI Wiest) explicitly states in a ScienceDaily press release: "Since we don't know of another (i.e., classical) way that anesthetic binding to microtubules would generally reduce brain activity and cause unconsciousness, this finding supports the quantum model of consciousness." This is a claim from absence of known classical mechanism, not positive evidence for quantum mechanism.

**The clean experiment (not yet done):** The definitive test would be: (a) use an inhalational anesthetic that does NOT bind MTs but that acts on GABA/NMDA/etc. receptors; (b) test whether epoB delays LORR under that anesthetic. If epoB has no effect, the MT-stabilizing effect is specific to isoflurane's MT binding (supporting quantum model). If epoB delays LORR under any anesthetic, the classical synaptic/transport protection route becomes more likely. This experiment has not been published.

### 3.6 Commentary and replication status

- **Wiest commentary (Neurosci Conscious, 2025):** Full review article by PI Wiest presenting this result as anchor evidence for a quantum MT substrate of consciousness.
- **April 2026 Frontiers Human Neuroscience editorial (Wiest and Gupta):** Cites Khan et al. 2024 as part of the accumulating evidence for MT involvement in anesthetic mechanisms.
- **Wiest 2026 mouse replication (Neuropharmacology):** Confirms the effect in mice, establishing cross-species validity, though with smaller effect size.
- **2026 Reddit/neuroscience discussion (r/neuro, May 2026):** A new paper reported to connect MT-stabilizing drugs, delayed unconsciousness, and microtubule tryptophan network dynamics — citing 12–15% reduction in energy transfer through MT tryptophan networks by anesthetics, paralleling the Kalra in-vitro finding.
- **CRITICAL CAVEAT — Huang et al. (2026) BMC Anesthesiology, DOI 10.1186/s12871-025-02956-9:** Prolonged (>2 weeks) administration of multiple MT-modulating drugs in male CD1 mice produced *directionally inconsistent* effects on isoflurane sensitivity. Epothilone D and vinblastine **increased** sensitivity (leftward EC50 shift, 95% CI for EC50: 0.75 [0.73, 0.77] for epoB-D), morphine shifted sensitivity the other way, and paclitaxel only slightly changed it. **This is opposite directionality to Khan/Wiest's acute-dosing rat result** and constitutes a key replication caveat. Plausibly explained by methodological differences (prolonged vs. acute dosing, mouse vs. rat, EC50 vs. fixed-concentration latency). **The clean statement is therefore not "microtubule stabilization always causes anesthetic resistance" but rather "microtubule-targeting manipulations measurably alter anesthetic sensitivity, and the direction depends on the drug, regimen, and model."**
- **Yu et al. (2026) Experimental Cell Research 457:114938** (DOI: 10.1016/j.yexcr.2026.114938): epoB counteracts isoflurane-induced slowing of swimming in *Tetrahymena pyriformis*, extending the effect to single-celled eukaryotes and broadening the phylogenetic case.
- No independent same-design rat replication of Khan/Wiest 2024 has been published as of May 2026.

## 4. Orch-OR Status 2026

### 4.1 Trajectory since the 2014 critique cycle

The 2014 Penrose-Hameroff response in Physics of Life Reviews acknowledged some critics' points while defending the core hypothesis. Since then, the theory has gained empirical traction in a way it had not between 2000 (Tegmark) and 2014. Key post-2014 developments that have changed the conversation:

- Tegmark's decoherence critique was "never conclusive" (Wiest and Gupta, 2026); Hagan et al. (2002) correction has been widely accepted as weakening the most powerful objection.
- Empirical evidence for quantum effects in MTs at room temperature (Kalra 2023; Babcock 2024) established physical plausibility where before there was only theoretical argument.
- **The Reimers-McKemmish 2009 critique** ("Tubulin lacks the conformational switching kinetics required by Orch-OR") **has not been directly refuted.** Orch-OR proponents have effectively *moved the qubit* from tubulin conformational states to electronic states in aromatic π-clouds, which weakens but does not address the original critique. The framework's vocabulary forces Orch-OR proponents to be explicit about *which s* is the candidate state — conformational versus electronic — with different τ_R observables for each.
- The anesthesia-MT connection (Khan 2024; Wiest 2026) provided behavioral pharmacological support.
- Orch-OR has been extended to connect with active inference theory (Wiest and Puniani, 2025), and with the quantum-classical complexity frameworks (Sergi et al., 2025).

**The theory has not gained acceptance in mainstream neuroscience.** It remains peripheral to the dominant paradigms (global workspace theory, integrated information theory, predictive coding). What has changed is that dismissing it as obviously wrong has become harder.

### 4.2 Current defenders and critics

**Strongest current defenders:**
- **Stuart Hameroff (University of Arizona):** Still the principal advocate. Active on social media, citing each new result as support. His January 2026 Twitter/X post: "Kalra et al. showed 10^-8 secs coherence times experimentally... Recent evidence includes 2024 superradiance in microtubules and anesthetic binding delaying unconsciousness in rats."
- **Michael Wiest (Wellesley College):** Has emerged as the most systematic experimental supporter. His 2025 Neurosci Conscious paper is the most rigorous defense of a quantum MT substrate published to date, grounded in actual experimental work from his own lab.
- **Bandyopadhyay (NIMS, Japan):** Provides the resonance-hierarchy and DDG frameworks, though his work has not been independently replicated.

**Strongest critics:**
- **Max Tegmark (MIT):** His 2000 paper remains the anchor citation for decoherence skepticism. The April 2026 Popular Mechanics article notes "MIT physicist Max Tegmark crunched the numbers and found that any quantum effect in microtubules would unravel in about 10^-13 seconds at body temperature." Tegmark has not published a major update to his 2000 analysis in response to recent experiments.
- **Ma and Wang (Xiamen University, 2026):** Represent the most systematic recent critical assessment, concluding that Orch-OR "tends to remain at the level of an intuition" philosophically and that its empirical grounding is limited to "simplified laboratory systems rather than living human brains."
- **Lea Gassab (University of Waterloo):** From a quantum information science standpoint, finds microtubules speculative compared to Posner molecules. Her assessments (Entropy 2025; Popular Mechanics 2026) consistently note the absence of evidence linking MT quantum effects to brain behavior specifically.

### 4.3 Modifications proposed since 2014

1. **Shift from conformational superpositions to tubulin excitation dipole states** (Gassab et al., 2025): More recent iterations emphasize π-electron delocalization and excitation dipole superpositions rather than mechanical conformational flips, aligning better with the quantum optical evidence from Kalra and Babcock.

2. **Downscaling of coherence requirements:** If Orch-OR events occur at 10 MHz rather than gamma (~40 Hz), coherence needs to persist for only 10^-7 s — well within the experimentally supported range. This "frequency-shift" escape from the timing critique has been articulated in several 2025 papers (Sergi et al.; Quantum-Classical Complexity review).

3. **Integration with active inference (Wiest and Puniani, 2025):** Proposed that quantum OR dynamics in MTs provide a natural physical substrate for Friston's active inference model, one of the most influential contemporary computational theories of brain function.

4. **Time crystal framing (Saxena et al., 2020; Bandyopadhyay lab):** MTs proposed as "polyatomic time crystals" — periodic structures in time exhibiting non-equilibrium self-organized dynamics across scales from THz to Hz. This framing appeared in a Journal of Applied Physics 2022 paper from the NIMS group.

### 4.4 Competing quantum-consciousness theories

**Matthew Fisher's nuclear spin / Posner molecule model:**
- Proposed 2015. Phosphorus-31 nuclear spins as qubits; calcium phosphate Posner molecules (Ca9(PO4)6) as quantum memory storage protecting nuclear spin entanglement.
- Status 2026: Fisher presented ongoing experimental work at an MIT Consciousness and Reality colloquium (February 2025), stating his group is attempting to "close the loophole" of whether 31P nuclear spins could be operationally relevant. A PNAS paper (2025) found that lithium isotopes differentially alter calcium phosphate mineralization in vitro, consistent with but not confirming the Posner model.
- Gassab et al. (2025) identified Posner molecules as the "best bet" among the three models reviewed, based on the theoretical robustness of nuclear spin coherence to thermal noise.
- The Ma and Wang (2026) Frontiers review describes Fisher's theory as "scientifically intriguing but philosophically incomplete."

**Kerskens MRI entanglement model (Trinity College Dublin):**
- The 2022 JPC paper by Kerskens and Pérez claimed heartbeat-evoked non-classical MRI signals as evidence for a macroscopic quantum entangled state in conscious brains. The Wiest (2025) paper treats this as "strong evidence." The Ma and Wang (2026) review notes the controversy is "unresolved" and independent replications have not confirmed it. The signal's correlation with memory and consciousness state remains the most intriguing aspect; the quantum interpretation remains contested.

**CEMI field theory (McFadden) / TRAZE model (Keppler):**
- McFadden's Conscious Electromagnetic Information field theory: synchronized neural firing creates a coherent EM field that constitutes consciousness. Discussed in Gassab et al. (2025) as a second major model family. Lacks direct experimental confirmation.
- Keppler's TRAZE (Theory of Resonant Amplification of Zero-point modEs): cortical microcolumns couple to the electromagnetic zero-point field; glutamate-ZPF resonance initiates conscious states. Published in Frontiers of Human Neuroscience (2026). Seen as compatible with MT hypothesis by Wiest and Gupta (2026).

## 5. Bandyopadhyay Lab Update

### 5.1 Current status of Bandyopadhyay's program (NIMS, Tsukuba)

Anirban Bandyopadhyay (Senior Researcher, National Institute for Materials Science, Japan) has been the most prolific publisher in the microtubule resonance space. His SAMURAI publications list shows:

**2022–2024 highlights:**
- **Polyatomic time crystals of brain neuron-extracted microtubules (Saxena, Singh, Sarkar, Sahoo, Ghosh, Krishnananda, Hill, Bandyopadhyay; J. Applied Physics, 2022):** Demonstrated a "polyatomic time crystal" in neuron-extracted MT nanowires using dielectric resonance and quantum optics. ### 5.3 DDG device and THz brain measurement

The Dodecanogram is the Bandyopadhyay group's primary tool for brain measurement. It uses RLC resonator arrays and logic analyzers to capture potential changes in the brain surface from Hz to THz via dielectric resonance — a fundamentally different method than THz beam illumination spectroscopy. Claims:
- 12 discrete frequency bands (vs. 5 in standard EEG)
- Cognitive/perceptual bursts occur at 200–700 nanoseconds
- In 24/7 monitoring of an "artificial organic brain replica" over a year and multiple human subjects

## 6. Anesthetic-Tubulin Hypothesis

### 6.1 What the field actually accepts

There is now substantial consensus that anesthetics bind to microtubules. This is established from:
- Pan et al. (2008): volatile anesthetics bind to cytoskeletal MTs
- Futterer et al. (2004) and Kalenka et al. (2007): MT binding/altered protein expression after anesthetics
- Craddock et al. (2015, 2017): quantum chemical modeling of binding sites

What is contested is whether MT binding is causally relevant to anesthesia-induced unconsciousness (as opposed to being an epiphenomenal binding among many promiscuous hydrophobic sites).

### 6.2 Alternative mechanisms of anesthetic action

**GABA potentiation:** Isoflurane and most volatile anesthetics potentiate inhibitory GABA_A receptors, increasing chloride flux and hyperpolarizing neurons. Mice with nonfunctional GABA_A receptors are "somewhat" resistant to isoflurane but not immune. This is the mainstream mechanism for inhalational agents.

**NMDA blockade:** Ketamine acts primarily via NMDA receptor antagonism. The MT hypothesis does not directly address ketamine consciousness (a notable gap — see Section 6.4).

**Mitochondrial inhibition (Complex I):** Isoflurane inhibits mitochondrial complex I, reducing ATP production and neuronal activity. A plausible classical route to unconsciousness independent of MT binding.

**Global workspace disruption:** MIT (2024) propofol work (published in Neuron) shows propofol induces unconsciousness by disrupting the brain's dynamic stability — causing escalating neural instability via disinhibition. This is a purely classical mechanism for a non-isoflurane anesthetic.

**VLPO activation:** Isoflurane directly activates sleep-promoting hypothalamic circuits.

### 6.3 The "clean experiment" (not yet done)

The hypothesis requires: find an anesthetic that abolishes consciousness but demonstrably does NOT bind to tubulin (or binds to different sites), administer epoB, and test for LORR delay. If no delay, MT binding is specifically required. If delayed, something more general is happening (perhaps epoB protects synaptic vesicle transport regardless of anesthetic target). Ketamine is a candidate "doesn't bind MT" control, though its MT binding affinity requires verification. No such experiment has been published.

### 6.4 The Hameroff anesthetic-tubulin hypothesis specifically

Hameroff's claim: anesthetics cause unconsciousness by disrupting a delicate entangled collective quantum state of neural MTs. The susceptibility of the coherent quantum state to disruption by relatively weak binding explains why anesthetics specifically abolish consciousness at moderate doses despite binding promiscuously to many targets. This explanation has an elegant internal logic: if consciousness is a quantum coherence in MTs, then any ligand that disrupts MT quantum coherence (without necessarily affecting classical neural activity) would produce selective loss of consciousness. The Craddock quantum chemical modeling (2015, 2017) supports the binding affinity correlation.

**Current evidence for this specific claim:** The Kalra (2023) finding that quantum optical effects in MTs are dampened by anesthetics is the most direct support. The Khan et al. (2024) behavioral result is consistent but admits classical interpretation. The claim that "any ligand that disrupts MT quantum coherence would selectively abolish consciousness" remains unfalsified but untested with clean controls.

## 7. Framework Application — Microtubule Residence-Pathology

### 7.1 PCI Framework Paper 14 vocabulary (from Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20145811)

The framework introduces:
- **R(s, c) = τ_R(s) × π_c** — two-axis residence-time observable combining temporal dwell τ_R in state s with compartmental occupancy probability π_c
- **λ_exit(s, c) = 1/τ_R(s, c)** — exit-rate variable, capturing return-path-machinery capacity
- **Normalized form Δ_R(s, c) = log(τ_R/τ_0) + log(π_c/π_0)** — substrate-portable
- **Pathology:** R escaping substrate-specific functional residence bounds on either axis
- **Return-path-machinery failure** as the grammatical framing of pathology

Paper 14's eight demonstrated substrates include receptor pharmacology, proteostasis, brain network dynamics, and memory — but NOT quantum biology or microtubules explicitly.

### 7.2 Natural analogs in the MT coherence literature

The microtubule quantum coherence literature generates several observables that map naturally onto the PCI vocabulary:

**Temporal dwell τ_R:**
- Superradiant (bright) state lifetime in Babcock tryptophan network: ~hundreds of femtoseconds
- Subradiant (dark) state lifetime: up to **tens of seconds** (Babcock et al., 2024)
- Kalra electron-migration coherence time: ~10^-8 s (cited by Hameroff, 2026)
- Hagan-corrected Orch-OR coherence estimate: 10^-5 to 10^-4 s
- QED cavity model (Mavromatos et al., 2025): ~10^-6 s
- Saxena et al. (2020) experimental observation: 10^-6 to 10^-4 s

The T1 (energy relaxation) and T2 (dephasing) vocabulary from quantum computing maps directly to τ_R. In fact, the standard definition of coherence time — "the lifetime of usable quantum information" — is precisely τ_R(s) for the state s = "MT coherence."

**Compartmental occupancy π_c:**
- The fraction of MTs in a neuron (or neural population) that are simultaneously in a coherent quantum state — this is the compartmental occupancy probability in the PCI vocabulary.
- Under normal (awake) conditions: π_c at some baseline level.
- Under anesthesia: π_c reduced — fewer MTs in the coherent compartment.
- Under epoB stabilization: π_c maintained or increased under isoflurane challenge.

**Exit-rate λ_exit = 1/τ_R:**
- Under anesthesia: λ_exit increases — the anesthetic raises the exit rate from the coherent MT state, shrinking τ_R and driving π_c down.
- This is precisely the Hameroff "disruption of delicate quantum coherence by relatively weak anesthetic binding" — reframed as a λ_exit elevation event.

**Pathology:**
- Consciousness-loss corresponds to R(s, c) falling below the functional bound on the τ_R axis (coherence lifetime too short) or the π_c axis (insufficient population fraction in coherent state), or both.
- In the epoB experiment: epoB raises τ_R (stabilizes MTs), increasing R and keeping the system above the functional threshold longer under isoflurane challenge.

### 7.3 What a PCI-vocabulary microtubule instance would look like

A Paper 16 application would define:
- **State s:** "MT quantum coherent" (vs. "MT incoherent/classical")
- **τ_R(s):** coherence lifetime (experimentally measured in the Kalra/Babcock range)
- **π_c:** fraction of neural MTs simultaneously in coherent state (not yet directly measurable in vivo)
- **λ_exit = 1/τ_R(s,c):** decoherence rate — elevated by anesthetic binding, reduced by epoB
- **Functional bounds:** τ_0 = the minimum coherence lifetime needed for conscious function (the Orch-OR threshold or analogous); π_0 = minimum population fraction
- **Pathology (unconsciousness):** Δ_R = log(τ_R/τ_0) + log(π_c/π_0) < 0

This formulation makes several things explicit that the existing literature obscures:
1. The two-axis structure distinguishes between a failure of coherence lifetime (τ_R axis) and a failure of coherent population (π_c axis) — they predict different pharmacological interventions.
2. The "return-path machinery" maps onto: endogenous quantum error correction mechanisms (if any), MT-associated proteins (MAPs) that protect coherence, metabolic energy pumping (the Fröhlich mechanism), or the cell's protein quality-control apparatus that replaces denatured tubulin.
3. Pathology as "return-path-machinery failure" rather than "quantum decoherence" shifts the therapeutic framing: instead of asking "how do we prevent decoherence?" the question becomes "what machinery should be returning the system to the coherent state, and why isn't it working in pathological consciousness disorders?"

### 7.4 Is the Bandyopadhyay resonance hierarchy a coupled-system observable?

The triplet-of-triplets hierarchy (THz → GHz → MHz → kHz → Hz) can be read in PCI vocabulary as a **nested coupling cascade**: each scale is coupled to the adjacent scale, with a characteristic τ_R at each level. Coupling failure at any level would prevent the quantum event from being "amplified" up to macroscopic neural timescales. In this reading:
- τ_R at the THz level (sub-picosecond to nanosecond): the initial quantum event
- τ_R at the GHz level: coupling of THz event into MT resonance mode
- τ_R at the kHz level: MT resonance reaching neuron-scale electrical activity
- τ_R at the Hz level: neural-scale consciousness correlate

Pathology could occur at any coupling step — a "return-path machinery failure" that prevents the quantum information from traversing the cascade. This is a genuinely novel framing that the existing Bandyopadhyay literature does not articulate.

## 8. Critical Assessment — Honest Field Status

### 8.1 Is the program "becoming experimentally grounded"?

The Debrief (May 13, 2026) headline frames the Frontiers Psychology review as evidence that "quantum theories of consciousness are becoming more experimentally grounded." This framing is partially accurate but substantially overstated.

**What is accurate:** Compared to 2020, there are now more experiments (Babcock 2024, Khan 2024, Wiest 2026, Kalra 2023, Gutierrez 2021) that generate findings consistent with quantum MT hypotheses. These are real experiments with real results that are not obviously artifacts. The decoherence dismissal has weakened. The field is generating testable predictions.

**What is overstated:** Every result is either in vitro, ex vivo, or indirect. No result directly measures quantum coherence in an MT in a conscious animal. The anesthesia-delay result (the strongest behavioral finding) admits classical interpretation. The MRI entanglement result (Kerskens 2022) remains unreplicated. The resonance hierarchy (Bandyopadhyay) remains unconfirmed by independent labs. "Experimentally grounded" implies a body of replicated, controlled, mechanistically understood results; the current literature is better described as "empirically suggestive."

### 8.2 Honest one-paragraph field status (May 2026)

The microtubule-quantum consciousness program has produced a cluster of results since 2020 that make dismissal difficult and serious investigation warranted. The strongest evidence comes from quantum optical measurements in purified MT preparations (superradiance at room temperature; UV exciton propagation exceeding classical predictions; anesthetic dampening of these effects) and from behavioral pharmacology showing a specific, large-effect-size delay in anesthetic-induced unconsciousness when MTs are stabilized. These results are real, but all involve either in vitro preparations or indirect behavioral readouts — no direct measurement of quantum coherence in a neural MT in a living conscious animal exists. The field's primary theory (Orch-OR) has been updated to accommodate decoherence constraints but has not closed the philosophical gap between "quantum processes in MTs" and "these quantum processes constitute or cause consciousness." Competing theories (Fisher nuclear spin, Kerskens MRI entanglement) offer independent evidence pathways but face their own empirical deficits. The honest summary: quantum consciousness research is no longer obviously wrong, but it is nowhere near obviously right.

### 8.3 What the field needs to see

1. **In-vivo MT quantum state measurement** (direct, not pharmacological inference) in a conscious vs. anesthetized animal
2. **Independent replication** of Bandyopadhyay resonance hierarchy
3. **Independent replication** of Kerskens MRI entanglement signal
4. **The "clean anesthetic" control** (epoB + non-MT-binding anesthetic)
5. **Isotope substitution experiment** in intact neural tissue (predicted by quantum models, not yet done)
6. **Pre-registered, multi-center replication** of Khan et al. epoB result

## 10. Prior-Art Audit for the PCI Framework

### 10.1 Has anyone published a residence-time or coupling-failure account of microtubule coherence?

**Answer: No.** A comprehensive search of the 2018–2026 literature on quantum consciousness and microtubule biophysics finds no paper using the terms "residence time," "dwell time" (in the exit-kinetics sense), "coupling failure," "return-path machinery failure," or "exit rate" to describe microtubule quantum coherence states. The PCI vocabulary — R(s,c) = τ_R × π_c with λ_exit as the return-path capacity observable — is novel applied to this substrate.

The closest approximations:

1. **Decoherence time (τ_dec):** The literature uses "decoherence time" or "coherence lifetime" to describe how long a quantum state persists before environmental coupling destroys it. This is conceptually related to τ_R but is not paired with a compartmental occupancy dimension (π_c), and it is not operationalized as an exit rate paired with a return-path capacity. The decoherence literature treats τ_dec as a physical constant of the system, not a dynamically modulated observable paired with a return mechanism.

2. **T1 / T2 relaxation times (quantum computing vocabulary):** Used in the Quantum Biology Framework paper (vixra, 2026) to describe MT quantum states. T1 (relaxation/energy loss) and T2 (dephasing) are closely analogous to τ_R. However, no paper uses this vocabulary within a coupling-failure / return-path-machinery framing, and none pair it with a π_c occupancy dimension.

3. **Coherence lifetime in superradiance papers (Babcock 2024):** The Babcock paper explicitly provides numbers (bright states: hundreds of femtoseconds; dark states: tens of seconds) that function as τ_R values. But the paper makes no residence-pathology argument and does not frame abnormal dwell times as pathological.

4. **Frohlich condensation "pumping" rate:** The metabolic energy input that sustains Frohlich condensates could be read as an analog of the "return-path machinery" λ_exit reciprocal. However, the framing is "energy pumping rate" rather than "return-path capacity," and no paper explicitly pairs it with a pathology criterion.
