## §8.6.2 Intersubjective negative pressure (CUT from Paper 14 v1)

**Reason for deferral:** Phenomenon is real and the framework's
prediction is sharp, but the empirical literature (dual-EEG
inter-brain beta-phase coupling during withheld continuations) is
not mature enough to support this as a worked example in a cross-
substrate journal paper. Reserves the strongest version of the
claim for a paper that can present primary data or commit to a
specific replication of an existing dual-EEG result.

**Cut text (verbatim):**

> **8.6.2 Intersubjective negative pressure.** Conversational
> coupling provides the fastest-timescale instance of residence-
> pathology in the framework's archive. When a speaker stops
> mid-sentence without warning and holds the silence while staying
> engaged, both parties feel a pressure that accumulates with hold
> duration, is produced by withholding rather than action, and
> discharges through one of three channels (resumption, alternate-
> channel release such as laughter, or decoherence). The candidate
> formalization is P_NP(t) = ∫ f(τ_actual - τ_design) ds on a
> coupled coherence substrate. The dual-EEG experimental prediction
> is that inter-brain beta-phase coupling increases during held
> pauses — counter-intuitive, because silence is often framed as
> relaxation, but predicted by the framework because the
> prediction-machinery loading of both brains against a withheld
> continuation would manifest as intensified coupling. See the
> technical archive memo `negative_pressure_intersubjective.md` for
> the full treatment.

**Where it belongs:** The embodied-PCI / blind-fluency follow-on
paper, which already has the operational vocabulary for fast-
timescale coupling and can devote a full results section to the
dual-EEG prediction with appropriate experimental design.

## §8.6.3 Public-epistemic substrate using "missing scientists" example (CUT from Paper 14 v1)

**Reason for cut (not just deferral):** The framework's claim about
emergent canal-deepening in public information ecosystems is
correct and worth keeping in the paper. The specific "missing
scientists" example, however, carries conspiracy-theory valence
that academic reviewers react to viscerally before evaluating the
structural argument, threatening to lose the room on substrate-7
just as the cross-substrate argument is converging. Claude
flagged this as a desk-rejection risk; ChatGPT Pro independently
recommended cutting.

**The structural claim survives in Paper 14 v1 as a NEW §8.6.2,
re-anchored to Cinelli et al. 2020 / Vosoughi-Roy-Aral 2018 / Bail
et al. 2018 (echo chambers, rumor velocity, partisan media
polarization). Those substrates have actual research literatures
and present the same residence-pathology pattern without the
political ambient.**

**Cut text (verbatim, for archival only):**

> **8.6.3 Public-epistemic attractor formation.** The framework's
> principle extends to information-ecosystem substrates where the
> attractor-landscape geometry is shaped collectively rather than
> individually. Emergent canal-deepening in public information
> ecosystems — patterns where new events are recruited into a
> pre-existing interpretive attractor regardless of base rates —
> is the public-epistemic instance of residence-pathology. The
> recent "missing scientists" discourse cluster (2024–2026)
> provides a case study in emergent landscape deepening without
> explicit steering: each new death, regardless of actual cause, is
> pulled into the conspiracy-shaped attractor, deepening the canal
> and making future retrievals more likely. The framework's
> technical archive treats this substrate in detail; here we note
> only that the substrate is coherent with the residence-pathology
> principle and fits the two-axis observable (τ_R = dwell time on
> the interpretive frame; π_c = compartmental occupancy in the
> narrative-shape ecosystem).

**Where the discourse-cluster instance could live:** A specialty
venue on emergent narrative-attractor dynamics in social media, or
a book chapter, or as a sidebar example in a non-academic essay.
The framework's claim about it is correct; the journal-paper
context is wrong for it.
